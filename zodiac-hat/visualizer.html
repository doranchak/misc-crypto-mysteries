Result

Yes, it holds a real message, enciphered with the Z408 key (the Hardens' solution). Read row by row, left to right, the 24 symbols decode to THE HOLLYWOOD HILL HAVE EYES, i.e. “The Hills Have Eyes” with the S of HILLS missing (24 symbols fit only if it is dropped; probably a maker's slip or a space-saving shortcut). The Z340 key gives no English in any reading order.

What was tested

Z408 key, 9 reading orders (rows, columns, snake, reversed, bottom-up)Row-major left→right gives English. Automatic reading with ambiguous glyphs scored −6.0 per quadgram versus about −5.7 for shuffled glyphs; after fixing two glyphs by eye (reversed D → O, reversed C → V) the whole line reads THEHOLLYWOODHILLHAVEEYES.
Z340 key (Oranchak–Blake–Van Eycke, 2020), same ordersBest −5.6 per letter, gibberish such as “TRPARRORWLEBDDEROBLRVTWS”; shuffled glyphs reach −5.5. No message.
Direct decryption without a keyNot needed. With only 24 symbols (19 distinct) an unkeyed solution would not be reliable anyway.
Chance?A 24-letter coincidence of this kind (five words, “HOLLYWOOD”, “HAVE EYES”) is far outside what random glyph shuffles give.
