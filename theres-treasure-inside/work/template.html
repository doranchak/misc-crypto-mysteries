There's Treasure Inside: the Past and Future box puzzles

Jon Collins-Black, There's Treasure Inside (2024), chapter XXVI, “The Past and Future Box”: the word search and cryptogram from the 17-page excerpt book-excerpts.pdf. Report built 26 Nov 2026.

Cryptogram: solved exact

A plain one-for-one letter substitution, as in a newspaper cryptogram (22 letters used, no letter stands for itself). It decodes to a four-line rhyming poem:

From rock to field, from strong to stream,
past tree and posts to forest green,
arrive you now where beauty sheens.
It's under, over, in between.

Word search: solved every one of the 300 letters accounted for

The letters left over after the placed words are a hidden message. Each word (or pair of short words) is scrambled within its own run of leftover cells, and the message reads top to bottom in normal reading order:

THERE ARE FIVE CLUES IN THIS WORD SEARCH. BUT DO NOT TELL ANYONE. LINE THEM UP. THERE ARE NO SPECIAL BENDS OR TURNS. JUST REMAIN STEADY TO COMPLETE THE MISSION.

The message uses %%N_MSG%% cells plus 3 O's. The placed words cover %%N_WORD%% cells (25 words: numbers, NORTH/WEST, colours, terrain words). The cells left over inside the message area are 19 spare O's plus six letters that spell TN, NC, SC. Random letters (Q, Z and X included) appear only in the top row and the bottom two rows.

Location: not confirmed box not reported found

The puzzles are solved, but they don't produce a single spot that can be checked from a desk. Two leads hold up when checked against the grid:

  • Lake Tahoe, at the bend in the California–Nevada border (39°00′N 120°00′W), my strongest lead. The number words that read backward are NINE, TWELVE, THREE and ZERO. The only valid reading inside the US is 39 / 120. NORTH and WEST also read backward. The commemorative coin shows two straight lines that meet in a small circle at %%COIN_ANG%%°, and the border bends inside the lake at %%OBLIQUE%%°. The chapter text adds “a state of gold” (California's nickname is the Golden State).
  • The Tennessee / North Carolina / South Carolina corner, the author's childhood region. The hand-placed letters TN, NC and SC sit in roughly their real map positions. But the grid has no number words that could give coordinates there.

Status: the fan site Mysterious Writings reports the Pokémon box found in Arkansas, announced 25 Nov 2026. It says the Past and Future box is still unfound. See Hunt status and sources.

3. Cryptogram

%%INTERLINEAR%%

Key

%%KEYTABLE%%
  • The solve starts from the obvious pieces. X is the most common letter (18 times), so X = E. Rl'p = IT'S, jxldxxy = BETWEEN, and cyqxh, iexh = UNDER, OVER. The rest follows.
  • “This” (capitalised) and “this” are both cipher for FROM. The fact that they look like English is a coincidence.
  • The key has no keyword pattern. It contains the swapped pairs c↔u and n↔y, and the cycles (h r i o) and (m p s). Plain letters J, Q, X, Z and cipher letters f, g, k, w are unused.
  • The surrounding text (Grandpa counting cars from a lawn chair, “as happy as if basking in the heavens”) is the hint that this is a newspaper cryptogram. Tahoe theorists read “heavens” as Heavenly Mountain Resort. An NC-side equivalent would be Grandfather Mountain, but that is only my speculation.

4. The commemorative coin

It can be read as a minimalist clock (fitting for “past and future”). It also matches the one bend in the CA–NV border. That bend lies inside Lake Tahoe, which would be the small circle. The two angles differ by about 3.5°. A random angle would land that close only about 4% of the time, so this supports the Tahoe lead but doesn't prove it. The coin is inside the box, so this is a clue to the box's location only if Seth Gould designed it that way.

5b. Tennessee / North Carolina / South Carolina deliberate, but region-level only

%%STATES%%
  • Clearly deliberate: every other spare cell in the message area is an O. These six cells instead spell TN (R10C9–10), NC (R10C12–13) and SC (R11C13–14), laid out roughly like the map: TN west of NC, SC south of NC.
  • The T of STATE (R10C11) sits exactly between TN and NC, like a state line. WATER, DIVIDE(R) and PEAKS fit the Blue Ridge and the Eastern Continental Divide.
  • Collins-Black grew up in North Carolina. Press coverage describes boyhood gem hunting at the Emerald Hollow Mine at the foot of the Brushy Mountains, and Kile's article places him near Statesville. That is the “past” in the blurb.
  • Limit: coordinates in that region (35–36°N, 76–84°W) would need SEVEN, EIGHT, THIRTY or EIGHTY, and none of those is in the grid. The six letters name a region, not a spot, and could stand for the author's past rather than where the box is. Community versions: Kile's five clues “TN, NC, SC, STATE, DIVIDER” (Smokies, NC side), and Gary's Mushroom Rock, Signal Mountain TN theory (LILAC/SAPPHIRE/STATE/LINE/COKE). COKE is an accidental word (see note above).

5c. “Five clues … line them up”

The message says there are five clues and tells you to line them up. Nobody has pinned down which five, and I couldn't either. The placed words fall into tidy groups: numbers, NORTH/WEST, colours and gems (LILAC, LEMON, SAPPHIRE), terrain words (WATER, DIVIDE, PEAKS, QUARRY, DIRT, RAIL), and other words (COUNT, ROUND, WALK, SQUARE, (E)STATE). Some observations:

  • Read as places, “lines” could be lines of latitude and longitude, or a state line. “No special bends or turns” is in any case the ordinary word-search rule that words run straight.
  • Backward-reading words (13) and forward/down/diagonal words (12) split almost evenly. That fits a “past and future” theme: the backward words hold the 39/120 numbers plus NORTH and WEST.
  • COUNT, THREE and FIVE echo the Holy Hand Grenade passage in Monty Python and the Holy Grail, which the chapter names. Counting the spare O's gives NINETEEN, which is also in the grid. That may be playful design or chance.

6. The derived instructions

Put together, the two puzzles describe a walk rather than coordinates:

  1. Start at a rock, a named rock or boulder, and head to a field or meadow.
  2. From “strong” to a stream. “Strong” is unexplained. It could be a strong current or falls, or a place or feature named Strong.
  3. Past a tree and posts (fence posts, trail posts, or boundary monuments) into green forest.
  4. Arrive where something shines: water, a falls, or a view.
  5. The box is “under, over, in between”: tucked beneath and between things, or literally between two states if the state-line reading is right.

The author's Postscript rules, which he says contain no clues: nowhere dangerous, nothing under water, no boat needed, no climbing. Within 3 miles of a road. Never on private property except a public trail easement. Never buried, so don't dig. Don't search in snow or extreme heat. Know the local wildlife and hunting seasons, go with a companion, and carry water and a phone.
