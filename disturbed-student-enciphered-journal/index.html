Solved. It's a simple one-symbol-per-letter substitution (a homemade alphabet), and the plaintext is English.

    The key

    Each glyph is a base shape plus (usually) one or two dots. The dots belong to the letter; they are not separate symbols. For example a is a bracket with one dot and b is the same bracket with two dots. A long dash without a dot is the full stop, while the same dash with a dot above is i. Counts are over all legible words. q, x and z never occur. Click a card to highlight that letter on the pages.

    How it was solved

    1. Transcribe glyphs, not strokes. At first sight the text looks like loose lines and dots, which is why the OP suspected the dots were frequent letters. Zoomed in, the dots always sit in a fixed position inside or beside a stroke shape: [·, |·|, ⊓·, ⊔·, ·//, -̇, ȧ. So each shape-plus-dots unit is one letter. The word spaces are real, and a long dash with no dot ends sentences.
    2. Use the repeated short words. The two-glyph word 4ȧ appears four times. The three-glyph word Γı ȧ ⊔· appears three times on its own and twice more at the start of a four-glyph word. The prefix 4 ≐ :// starts seven words (three of them standalone). The first two words share ȧ as their 2nd glyph. Trying to / you(r) / the(m, ir, se) gives t, o, y, u, r, h, e.
    3. Fill in the gaps. With those six letters, ·/ ȧ ||· 4 = d‑o‑n‑t, =< ≐ :// ||· = w‑h‑e‑n, ¿· ȧ ⊓· :// = c‑o‑m‑e, and [: -̇ 中 ·/ |·| = b‑i‑r‑d‑s. That makes page 1 line 10 read "when the birds come to dry off their wings, you can go fly with them". After that every other word falls out. The rarer letters are confirmed by knows (k), previous / negativity / I've / every (v), just (j), and pen / problem / up (p).
    4. Check. All ~150 legible words on both pages decode to English with one fixed key. Each letter has one glyph (no homophones), there are no nulls, and the leftover marks are scribbles, a caret insertion and one Latin-letter annotation. The writer even comments on the pen change that's visible in the photo ("The previous pen was better").
