Cipher leads to a image

Source: https://www.reddit.com/r/ciphers/comments/1y0v8t5/cipher_leads_to_a_image/ · posted by u/Anxious-Anything-894 on r/ciphers

Result

Solved to the image and its hidden string: the 37 groups decode to https://imgur.com/a/mINZJSl → a PNG whose top pixel row hides NicEcoMplEte2 ("Nice complete 2").

Not decoded: the four lines of visible "nonsensical CODE" in the picture (see below). They are probably filler, but that is an inference, not something I could confirm (the Reddit thread could not be read from here, so I have not seen the poster's hints or any solution).

  1. Each group of 8 digits (only 1 and 2) is a byte with 1→0, 2→1. The first digit is always 1, so every byte has its top bit clear: 7-bit ASCII.
  2. Shift the letters back by 7 (Caesar, case kept; non-letters untouched). ptnbyjvt becomes imgurcom, oaawz becomes https.
  3. Junk characters sit where the URL's punctuation belongs (://, ., /, /) plus some at the ends. Reinserting the punctuation gives https://imgur.com/a/mINZJSl; the album (title "Whhat") exists and holds one image, https://i.imgur.com/qkhHnIc.png (2048×2048).
  4. The lowest bit of each colour value in the first 80 pixels of row 0 spells NicEcoMplEte2 (preceded by a carriage return, with a zero byte between characters). Every pixel after that is plain white 255.

Layer 2: the PNG

this Png contains nonsEnsical CODE:
Gdky5e
7e5usjf(p=d)jrsrsutefr7
gigkyjtur;$$+$+$
+_+3($

Only 559 distinct colours, no extra PNG chunks. The capital P and E in "Png" and "nonsEnsical" look deliberate, in the same style as the capital E's in the hidden string.

Tried on the four lines, no English result: Caesar on letters (all 25 shifts, whole text and lowercase-only), shifts over the 94 printable ASCII characters, single-byte XOR, Vigenere with keys nicecomplete / nicecomplete2 / png / code / nonsensical / imgur / https, every-2nd/3rd character, reversal, Atbash. The only curiosity: the letter pattern of jrsrsutefr matches "rededicate", but that does not extend to the rest, so I treat it as coincidence.
