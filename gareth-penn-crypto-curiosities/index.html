Gareth Penn's crypto curiosities

A catalog of every method Gareth Penn used, from 1981 to 2022, to "read" hidden messages in the Zodiac letters and to tie them to his suspect, Michael O'Hare. The catalog has interactive re-implementations and citations. After it comes an attack on the puzzles Penn posted on his blogs and on Twitter/X from 2010 to 2025.

Compiled 26 November 2026 from the collection "Zodiac/Solutions/Gareth Penn" (Times 17, The Second Power, Mensa Bulletin, The Cryptogram, blog posts, letters, FBI files, 2022 emails) plus web research. Penn's statements are reported as claims. Nothing here supports the accusation against O'Hare, who was never charged.

Summary of findings

  • Catalog. 192 distinct devices, grouped in 14 families and drawn from Times 17 (1987), the Mensa Bulletin (1985), The Cryptogram (1986), The Second Power (1999), the 2010–2011 blog and forum posts, and the 2022 essays Penn emailed you. Most have a working demo below. Penn's core engine never changed: turn words into numbers (Morse with dah = 1 and dit = 0 read as binary, or A = 1…Z = 26 with E = 3), then look for "meaningful" numbers (130 = BER, 202 = MIKE, 58 = O'HARE, 9745 = 9-7-45, 2068 = THE END…) in positions, dates, angles, distances and counts. Over 40 years he added grids, calendars, cubes and right triangles.
  • What "a cross between a haiku and a lottery ticket" means. The verse tweets are priority anagrams in the manner of Galileo and Hooke. Penn wrote about these in his first Zodiac article (California, Nov 1981, p.166) and in Times 17 (p.9). In 2010 he said explicitly that his own puzzles work like Hooke's CEIIINOSSSTTVV. The hidden content is a prediction: in every decodable case, a date and a place. "Insurance" is proof of priority, cashable only if the event happens. His "long trail of lottery tickets that didn't pay off" includes Copyright Office registrations such as "Papers relating to the impending suicide of Michael Henry O'Hare on 17 May 1989" and "The end: a prediction of the future" (2000). Details.
  • Solved: the March 2020 ticket. Eleven tweets (a 7×7 transposition, hex A424, 42020², ³, √, ∛, log₁₀, log₂, Columbine, three actors and Nina Foch, all born 20 April) say 20 April 2020, Point Reyes, California. That date is the anniversary of what Times 17 reads as O'Hare's conception date (4-20-42). Also solved: the September 2020 ticket. 9320 in bases 6–9 gives 3 Sept 2020, Plymouth, New York, and PLYMOUTH "adds up" to 130, Penn's number for O'Hare's mother.
  • Partly read: May 2020 (8 May; EHRENDINGEN = ALBANY VT = 97 = "9-7", the mother's birthday), "UAXTWA/Richmond" (the 1960 UA × TWA collision over Staten Island) and "abets" = BATES.
  • New structural result: the 2010 "Wednesday puzzle" is padded with self-cancelling WIZARD pairs, Penn's own reverse-alphabet null device (p ≈ 0.0003). So are two 2014 strings (p ≈ 0.002, 0.004). The remaining alphanumeric strings and the anagram verses are not decoded. By Penn's design and his own statement, they are unsolvable without his key. Constraints and negative tests are documented.
  • Writings you didn't have: The Ecphorizer archive (59 Penn articles, 1981–87, still online, about 20 of them on Zodiac ciphers or wordplay); "Portrait of the Artist as a Mass Murderer" (California, Nov 1981); a second pseudonym ("Garfield Shaeffer"); his 1982 letter to the Webster family; "Penn's Kooky Kodes" grids; a 2 Jul 2025 verse tweet missing from your list.     5. "A cross between a haiku and a lottery ticket": what Penn meant

    Short answer. Penn's tweets are priority anagrams: sealed, publicly timestamped predictions. The "haiku" is the visible verse. The "lottery ticket" is the hidden prediction, which (judging from every case that can be read) is a date and a place. "Insurance" means proof of priority. If the predicted event ever happened, Penn could reveal the plaintext and prove, from the tweet's timestamp, that he had foreseen it. That would vindicate his reading of the Zodiac texts and, through it, his identification of Michael O'Hare. "No particular recipient" fits a commitment: it is addressed to the public record, not to a person. "A long trail of lottery tickets that didn't pay off" is literally true. Penn has been registering dated predictions since 1988, and none came true.

    The evidence, in Penn's own words and records

    1. He described the device in print in 1981 and again in 1987. In his first Zodiac article ("Portrait of the Artist as a Mass Murderer", California, Nov 1981, p.166, as "George Oakes"): "Galileo's discovery of the phases of Venus and Huygens's discovery of a moon on Saturn were originally published as anagrams." In Times 17: "It was the practice of early modern astronomers to publish their discoveries in the form of anagrams… disguising their findings in a form from which only they could retrieve them. Galileo announced his discovery of the phases of Venus as an anagram." He quotes Galileo's poetic anagram Haec immatura a me jam frustra leguntur — oy, which is a line of verse that secretly encodes a claim, i.e. a haiku that is also a ticket. T17 p.9 (Part 1, PDF p.14); also p.12 ("The first information about the rings of Saturn was received on earth in the form of an anagram").
    2. He said his own puzzles work this way (2010). "Before anybody expends any more effort on these paltry little puzzles of mine, I would like to invoke the spirit of Robert Hooke, who proposed … a law of elasticity, which he published as an anagram: CEIIINOSSSTTVV. The only person who can testify as to the content of an anagram is the one who made it. Hooke intended to establish a claim to the principle by publishing it but to withhold the solution until a time that suited him. The Sunday and Wednesday puzzles (plus codicil) have the same purpose; I believe that they are completely unsolvable without knowing the context in which they are set." Letter to Zam, 16 Aug 2010 (collection: "2010 08 16 Letter to Zam from Gareth Penn").
    3. He thought the Zodiac built time capsules, and he builds puzzles himself. On the Zodiac: "advertising his identity the way he did was meant to be a kind of time-capsule. The puzzle was meant to be solved, but at some remove in time… maybe fifty years afterward." Letter to the Webster family, 3 Mar 1982 (web; see Sources). In the 1980s he set Mensa puzzles, including "a hellishly contrived puzzle involving a jigsaw puzzle, a Latin anagram, etc." Ecphorizer contributor notes. In 1983–86 he mailed his suspect coded items, such as two 17¢ stamps for "TIMES 17" and the return address "1623 West 117th Street" for MIKE O WEST. Ecphorizer #53 "Mike West", Jan 1986; T17 Parts 2–3.
    4. He treats prediction as the test of his theory, and offers bets. "The true test of a scientific theory is its ability to predict phenomena hitherto unseen… I am predicting that the box-score in this letter can be read as expressing the number 103 — before MIKADO. I am even willing to bet a substantial sum of money on it. Any takers?" T17 p.230 (Part 4, PDF p.1); repeated p.326. In The Second Power he bets again: "I am willing to bet a substantial sum of money that when the text [of the unpublished Examiner cover letter] is available, it will be found that there is an OHARE-redivision…" SP p.25 (PDF p.29). In the same booklet a calendar "commemorates the past while it predicts the future", and the Zodiac can use it as "a predictive tool". SP p.15 (PDF p.20). He also says "the only authority for the content of an anagram is the author." T17 p.54, Appx B p.351, Appx D p.368.
    5. He registered dated predictions with the U.S. Copyright Office, the paper equivalent of a timestamped tweet. These are from a list of Penn's registrations compiled by Mike Martin, which is in the collection: "Papers relating to the impending suicide of Michael Henry O'Hare on 17 May 1989" (TXu-354-164, registered 15 Dec 1988); "The Geometry of life and death" (TXu-383-923, 5 Sep 1989; application title "Resolution of the Zodiac mystery on 19 September 1989"); "Zodiac calendars" (TXu-406-808, 13 Feb 1990; application title "…resolution of the zodiac matter on or about 19 February 1990"); "8790" (TXu-430-480, registered 1 Aug 1990, a bare number that reads as the date 8-7-90); and "The end: a prediction of the future in six pages" (TXu-937-814, 22 Feb 2000). Mike Martin material in the collection; see the other-sources catalog. Every one of those dates passed without the predicted event. These are "the long trail of lottery tickets that didn't pay off."
    6. The 2020 tickets that can be decoded are exactly "date + place". On 21 March 2020 Penn tweeted a 49-letter cipher. Over the next ten days he tweeted the same information in about ten more disguises. It decodes to 20 April 2020 at Point Reyes, California (section 6.1). Under his own Times 17 reading, 20 April is the anniversary of O'Hare's supposed conception (KFV 26 → binary 42042 → "4-20-42", T17 Part 2). This fits Penn's late theme that "life is a circle" and BIRTH = DEATH ("WHEN I DIE I WILL BE REBORN"; Penn 2022, "340/793" and "Radian"). Later 2020 tickets name 3 September 2020, Plymouth, New York. "All the factors add up to one place." In Penn's own gematria PLYMOUTH adds up to 130, his number for O'Hare's mother (BER). Another ticket names 8 May and places that add up to 97, his "9-7", her birthday (section 6.2).
    7. Timing. The "Ida eats / ocarina vines…" tweet is dated 18 March 2022. That is the same day Penn emailed you his "sample" essay, after asking you to DM an email address. In that essay and the follow-ups ("340/793", "Radian") he sets out his late doctrine: the Zodiac "lets the calendar do his talking", texts are calendars, and BIRTH, DEATH and THE END are numerically "the same". A predicted date is the natural payload for a man who believed the Zodiac's writings forecast their author's own end.

    So what is he insuring?

    His claim to priority. In Penn's framework the Zodiac built his life and his writings as one numerical "opus" whose last term is the author's own end, on a date and at a place chosen to complete the design (life as a circle; a place whose name "adds up" to his mother). If Penn has decoded the design, he can predict the date and place. Publishing the prediction in plain text would let the author dodge it, so Penn hides it in an anagram-haiku and keeps the solution. If the event occurs, the tweet is the "lottery ticket" he can "cash in": proof that his method works and that O'Hare is the Zodiac. The wording of his registered titles ("impending suicide of Michael Henry O'Hare on 17 May 1989"; "Resolution of the Zodiac mystery on …"; "The end: a prediction of the future") suggests that is the kind of event he kept predicting. Caveat: the verse tickets of 2021–2025 have not been decoded (section 6.4), so their specific dates and places are unknown. Everything above follows from Penn's statements and the tickets that can be read. None of his predictions has come true, and nothing in this report supports Penn's accusation against O'Hare. It documents Penn's methods and claims only.

    6.3 The 2010–2018 alphanumeric puzzles

    Penn said these were "quite elementary" (Jan 2011), yet also "completely unsolvable without knowing the context" (Aug 2010). Here is what can be shown from their structure:

    • WIZARD-pair padding is real in three of them. Penn's WIZARD rule (T17 p.15) says letter pairs that mirror each other in the reverse alphabet (A/Z, B/Y, … GT, PK) "drop out" as "superfluous material". The test: cancel all such pairs and count what's left. For random letters the residue of a 70-letter string has median 24 (1st percentile 12). The Wednesday puzzle leaves only 8 (p ≈ 0.0003, 20,000 simulations). It contains almost exactly three of every letter, balanced against its mirror. 2014-04-28 leaves 6 of 34 (p ≈ 0.002) and 2014-05-27 leaves 7 of 39 (p ≈ 0.004). The other puzzles are consistent with chance, and the Sunday puzzle is the opposite (residue 26 of 32, p ≈ 0.9996), with English-like letters. Residues: Wednesday A C E F I J N T (the 2011 "codicil" 157116B adds a B); 2014-04-28 E E F K N Y; 2014-05-27 I I N T W Y Y.
    • Shared template (2013–2020). A "P" within the first 7 characters in 20 of 27 strings (within the first 12 in 24 of 27). Many rare letters (Q, K, X, J, Z), especially the WIZARD pairs P/K and Q/J. Near the end, a cluster "…RY", "…DY" or "…VY" followed by digits and an S/SE/SN. Keyboard-shift symbols ! @ # $ & * (= 1 2 3 4 7 8 on a US keyboard, Penn's own &# = 73 device, Penn 2022) appear in seven of them.
    • Versions. The archived gz4216 page labels the Dec 2014 string "(posted 21 December 2014, corrected 28 January 2015)". The string we have is the corrected one; the supplement BXLIGAXAL is from the same date. Each new puzzle overwrote the single blog post, and its HTML carries the Word metadata "Foxglove Press", the Times 17 imprint.
    • Tested and ruled out: base-36 numerals (no date substrings beyond chance); Penn's mixed Morse/binary reading of whole strings; single-number-in-several-bases for the Oct 2020 triples; Morse-binary dictionary readings of the digit strings (fluent nonsense at chance level, e.g. C5O1M7B9A8 → "KEY TO MOST ITEMS", shown only as a warning); 7×7 column reading of the three 49-character strings (2015-09-04, 2016-03-01, 2018-06-08).
    • Sunday puzzle (2010-06-19). The letters (digits removed) are T M W O X S E A V E E X X N T W O O X T J H R U X A X E N R Y E. "TWO" appears literally ("ntwoo"), and X may act as a separator (tmwo / seavee / ntwoo / tjhru / a / enrye). Penn called it "too difficult" and issued the Wednesday puzzle as a "simpler and easier version".
    • The codicil and the "last attempt". 157116B was appended to the Wednesday puzzle and then to "Lost? Try with hot soup!"; a solver's "Reginald Graham Wordsworth" was rejected ("I don't get it"). C5O1M7B9A8 (15 Feb 2011: "If this one doesn't work, I promise there will be no sequel") contains COMBA plus digits 5,1,7,9,8. Both remain unread.

    6.4 The anagram verses (Oct 2020 – Aug 2025)

    These are the "haiku" tickets. Firm observations:

    • They are built from a reusable stock of anagram phrases, each tied to a two-digit year. "egrets indeed" ↔ (10) and "satyr hanky panky" ↔ (98) in both 16 Mar 2021 and 2 Apr 2024. Phrases recur across tweets ("moist eponyms", "abhor mines", "ghost steam", "BLTs Iberian", "Amalie, is it time?", "Ida eats / ocarina vines / stairs starve / one sock civets", "frat crew / cited how / overdo Mel Gibson / Laverne greets"…). Earlier forms show the year explicitly: "pending scree (1997)", "Mao alien (1997)", "almond oil, pipette (2019)". So each phrase is one dated item, and a verse is an arrangement of items.
    • The stock persists for years. A verse not in your list, dated 2 Jul 2025 (found in the Wayback archive of the account), reuses four items from 12 Jul 2022 ("in a panic Sally", "flambé liners", "I do La Paz", "psalms unite"): In a panic, Sally / Poser rubs out / flambé liners, / horrid ASCII: / "I do, La Paz!" / Psalms unite / FanDuel, Nae Nae.
    • Exact hits: "Adore old" = EL DORADO (a place name; also a 1966 film); "abets" = BATES (the 1966 Riverside victim's surname). Matching every phrase against 237,000 world place names and 17,000 notable people (Wikidata) found nothing else exact.
    • Negative test: no verse is an exact anagram of a sentence in Penn's own 2020 format ("[DATE] month day year [AT] place state"), over every date 2000–2035 in several wordings (cardinal/ordinal day, "two thousand…"/"twenty twenty…") and every US place with population ≥ 500 (≈ 630,000 date phrasings × all places). So the verses are not simply scrambled Point-Reyes-style sentences.
    • Not Morse mnemonics: Penn did invent Morse mnemonics (DONUT JAM = the binary fraction of φ, T17 p.21, Part 1 PDF p.26), but the Morse-binary values of the verse lines are not dates, constants or recognizable numbers.
    • Why they resist solution: this is the point of a priority anagram. A 40–100-letter anagram has astronomically many readings, and Penn insisted that "the only authority for the content of an anagram is the author." Kepler famously "solved" Galileo's Saturn anagram wrongly. Without Penn's key, a reading can only be supported, not proven.

    6.5 Other 2020–2021 tweets

    • UAXTWA/Richmond (21 Nov 2020): read as "UA × TWA". On 16 Dec 1960 United Flight 826 and TWA Flight 266 collided over New York; TWA 266 fell on Miller Field, Staten Island, then the Borough of Richmond. This uses the same kind of external-fact clue as the Nina Foch films. Its role in a ticket is unknown.
    • Oct 2020 numbers: 4201244, 11231152 and 121795106 are all multiples of 17 (Penn's "TIMES 17"); 519474, 9131597 and 771663 are not. The chance that three of six random numbers are multiples of 17 is about 0.4%. They are not one number in several bases (tested 2–36), and they do not factor into sums of cubes or squares. "SFD6171579" contains 617, Penn's TIMEX / Boston area code.
    • "Of all bad ways to write a date, the baddest is 'I, Curt, do freight.'" (16 Aug 2021): a declared anagram of ICURTDOFREIGHT. A dictionary search gives many three-word readings. The most on-topic is "OCT THIRD FIGURE". Next day: "2 x 2 x 16 x 40" (= 2560).
    • "81/82 = 0.1" (22 Mar 2021): partly explained. 81/82 = 0.9878049… and the fractional part of √42020 = 204.9878045… (the March 2020 ticket) agree to six decimals. That is no accident: 42020 = 205² − 5, so √42020 ≈ 205 − 5/(2·205) = 205 − 1/82. The tweet, a year later, seems to refer back to the 4/20/20 number. What "= 0.1" means is unresolved.

    7. Notes, caveats, reproducibility

    • Why Penn's method "always works". Every word has two or more numerical values (alphabetic, Morse-binary, Roman). Morse bit-strings can be re-cut ("redivided") freely, even through letters. Numbers can be read as dates in several orders, as day-of-year, as clock times, as angles mod 360°, or after adding, subtracting ("X before Y"), dividing ("X over Y"), squaring and doubling. Positions can be counted in characters, Morse digits, rows, columns or millimetres. Any long text therefore contains thousands of hits on a vocabulary of a few dozen "significant" numbers. The decoder and redivision finder show this. Penn's odds ("1 in 1.25 million") price one pre-chosen hit, but he searched for hits after the fact. The FBI Laboratory said the same in 1981–83. It recognized his "'Wizard' and 'Binary Morse' substitutions" as standard techniques, but found "the bulk of the theory… based on speculation and a multitude of assumptions" (26 Jun 1981). Penn "selectively dissects the literature (often misquoting material) and presents results only if they corroborate the overall theme of his solution… the results are forced and inconsistent" (lab report of 24 Jan 1983 on his 119-page 1982 packet; FBI Zodiac file extracts in the collection).
    • Penn contradicts himself across sources (found while merging the catalog). Each version is presented as the designed reading:
      • Riverside Timex: stopped at 12:22 (T17) vs 12:24 (SP; 1224 = 72 × 17).
      • Calibers: .22, 9 mm, .38 ("22 before 9" → .38, T17) vs only .22 and 9 mm (GUN = 22 × 9, 2010/2022).
      • The "radian" letter: postmarked 28 June 1970 (6.28 = 2π, 1985) vs 29 June (6/29 = 37 × 17, 2022).
      • Day-of-year 314: assigned to the 340 mailing (T17, 1985) vs the Death Machine letter (SP).
      • The 32-symbol cipher bits cut as 20, 57, 58, 114, 117, 1682, 130, 112 (Cryptogram 1986) vs 20 … 117, 105, then UE BER OH (T17).
      • "& #": 73 Morse digits (T17) vs 73 radians = 4183 = BERTA M (SP) vs 7³ (2022).
      • O'Hare's student magazine "Cambridge 38", Feb 1964 (T17) vs "Cambridge 28", June 1964 (1985).
      • Parents married 1938 (T17) vs 1937 implied by "Me 37" (1985).
      • The "DES JULY AUG SEPT OCT 7" box-score: summed to 7 (T17) vs read as character #219 = MAY (2022).
      • The "a Friend" signature: π to 11 bits (FRIEND, 1985) vs about 0.29 of a radian (AFRI, 1987).
      • Morse polarity: "and vice versa" (Laube interview, 1981) vs dah = 1 everywhere later.
    • Penn's arithmetic slips (flagged on the cards): ME 43 = 427, not 350; "BM 80" whose tail 101000 is 40; five call times summed to 3744 that actually sum to 3743; "exactly 15 years" = 5478 days, not 5479; 37/27 = 1.370, not 1.38; four inches = 101.6 mm (only his 101.595 gives MIK.TIMES2); TIMEX printed as 627 (Ecphorizer #53; it is 617); DONUT JAM is 20 binary places of φ, not 19.
    • Privacy and fairness. Penn's books include private data (phone numbers, addresses, a Social Security number) that he "decoded". These are deliberately omitted here. Crimes are referred to by place and date only. Michael O'Hare was never charged. Penn's predictions about him never came true.

"SP" means The Second Power: printed pages = PDF−5 up to p.17 and PDF−4 from p.19, because printed p.18 is missing from the PDF and survives only as a separate Drive scan. Mensa Bulletin pages refer to the scans in the collection. The 2022 essays are cited by title.

Every example in the catalog was checked against the page images where it mattered, and the Morse/binary arithmetic was recomputed. Penn's own arithmetic errors are flagged on the cards (e.g. "ME 43" printed as 350; it is 427).
