# Times 17 (1987), Parts 4 and 5: catalog of Penn's methods

Source: Gareth Penn, *Times 17* (1987). Part 4 = PDF pp.1-78, printed pp.230-307. Part 5 = PDF pp.1-74, printed pp.308-380, plus the back cover.
OCR: `ocr/t17p4.v2.txt`, `ocr/t17p5.v2.txt`. I checked the garbled numbers, tables and figures against the page images.
Citation format: `T17 p.<printed> (Part N, PDF p.K)`. Square brackets around a page number, such as p.[236], mean the page carries no printed number and I inferred it from the neighbouring pages.

## Summary (what Parts 4-5 cover)
1. The end of the "telephone literature" (Dec 1984 to Dec 1986). Penn reads the clock times, intervals, sums and calendar-grid positions of one-ring hang-up calls as Morse-to-binary messages, and as pointers to a supposed burial site for Joan Webster near Fenn School in Concord, MA.
2. The maps. In "Someonell find her" he works USGS geometry on the Concord quadrangle (BM 130s, BM 123, UTM line 4705, Intersection 138, Point 127). He also reads the "bookends" BY...YB (349 vs 943 = binary "117 O") in the 340 cipher and the Confession Letter.
3. "Zodiac Identification Kit": the conventional evidence. Composite sketch, handwriting (the G of "Margoulies"), the ADL/Boise Cascade employment chronology, the résumé, the Harvard reunion entry, and three "angular artifacts" each with a USGS benchmark at the apex.
4. "End Game": a 10-move CHESS GAME decoded from the calendar grid of calls. It uses knights' moves, a phantom eighth "Noneday" column, KING = 662, the queen marked by BM numbers, and knights marked by 127 (MOM) and TIMES 17.
5. Narrative chapters: Mensa Bulletin "The calculus of evil" (about 500 letters), Frank Drake and Harvard, the FBI, the "Apology" hotline impostor, Charlie Ziarko digging at Fenn School (329 paper squares), and the Sentinel and Chronicle press.
6. "Antic Disposition": a rebuttal of Graysmith's astrology. Penn compares the murder dates with the 1985 airline disasters, recasts the cipher symbols as Greek letters, benchmark signs and the like, traces SLA/slá to five dictionaries, and reads 4 inches = 101.595 mm = MIK TIMES 2.
7. Part 5, "The End" through "On the Index": radian supplement 122°43' = O'Hare's birthday 1-22-43; THE END = 29 = 331 = 2068 = 01010; 1974 postmarks vs radian digits; the AFRI/REDWITH/RAGE binary fractions; RED PHANTOM, CITIZEN and SLA anagrams; the Hallowe'en Card (4-TEEN, eye-holes = 130, Scrabble score 79 = 9-7, the inverted-W = binary 331).
8. "Times 17": Penn's own 17x17 MAGIC SQUARE with sum 9745 (TIMES 17), which he matches to calendar-grid calls (429, 1146 = 573x2, 10:34, 4:47).
9. Appendices: A (what went wrong), B "A Zodiac Dictionary" (BER, BM, GENE, UE, 38, 100, MIKE, IK, π, 58, OH, radian, DM, RED, 150, MT, 117, Circle 7, NINE O, ZODIAC = GENEBERTA, 29), C chronology, D re-reading the victim claims (PEOPLE = 88, OCT = 38, SOUGHT VICTIM 12 = 02138), E telephone and real-estate numbers (6266 = MHO, 0451 = mirror, 62-left-of-66, 391, 79 ft, $9700, parcel #38), F letter texts.
10. A biographical footnote on decipherers (Valla, Champollion, Hrozny, Ventris), and the back cover (Exorcist Letter, "Me-37 / SFPD-0").

Morse-to-binary convention throughout: dah = 1, dit = 0. A number cannot begin with a dit, so words starting with E, I, S, H, U, V, F, A, R, L, W, J, P are not expressible.

## METHODS

### M: Clock time of a phone call → binary → Morse redivision
- Category: Morse/binary
- Rules: Take the clock time of a one-ring hang-up call as a decimal integer with the colon dropped (10:45 → 1045). a.m. and p.m. are ignored. Write it in base 2 and split the bits into Morse letters (1 = dah, 0 = dit). Leftover bits may be read as a binary numeral (see the "word + numeral tail" method). Penn also does "exhaustive redivision" to check for alternative words.
- Examples (exact):
  - 10:45 → 10000010101 → 1000 0 010 1 01 = **BERTA**. Penn calls it the first signal of BM's full given name.
  - 2:02 → 11001010 → 11 00 101 0 = **MIKE**.
  - 3:04 → 100110000 → 1 00 11 0 000 = **TIMES**. This came on 18 Dec 1984 as a receipt for Penn's 11"x17" mailing. Another came on 8 Jan 1987 as a receipt for his magic-square mailing.
  - 8:36 → 11 0 10 00 10 0 = **ME NINE**.
  - 12:16 → 10011000000 → 1 00 11 0 00 000 = **TIME IS**. The next call, at 12:22, completes the sentence "TIME IS 12:22", the time on the Timex watch left at the Riverside scene.
  - 10:00 → 1111101000 → 11 111 010 00 = **MORI** (Latin "to die"). "I did an exhaustive redivision, finding that it wouldn't spell anything else." He notes that DIE = binary 32, which is not expressible as a clock time.
  - 1:11 → 111 = **O**, read as "come full circle", "the end". The call fell on the 20th anniversary of the Confession Letter.
  - 12:35 → 10011010011 → 1 00 11 0 1001 1 = **TIMEX 1**: Riverside, his first crime.
  - 12:27 → 1 00 11 0 010 11 = **TIMER 3** (3 = BUS). Penn links it to the Death Machine Diagram: the timer centre is joined to the bus centre.
  - 12:45 → 1 00 11 0 11101 = **TIME 29** ("time for the end").
  - 12:04 → 10010110100 → 1 00 1 01 10 + 100 = **TITAN 4**, i.e. Antaeus, the 4th giant of Dante's Ninth Circle. Read as "admitting defeat".
  - 1:50 → 10010110 → 1 001 0110 = "digit UP" = **DIG IT UP**. Also 150 = TITAN.
  - 12:18 → 10011000010 → 1 00 11 0 000 10 = **TIMES 2**.
  - The 11:01 call (NINE 13 / OF IT) is in the "word + numeral tail" method.
- Claims: the calls are O'Hare's coded receipts and messages naming himself, his mother (BERTA, BM), the Timex/Riverside crime, and the Webster grave.
- Citations: T17 p.231 (Part 4, PDF p.2); p.233 (Part 4, PDF p.4); p.237 (Part 4, PDF p.8); p.240 (Part 4, PDF p.11); p.272 (Part 4, PDF p.43); p.279 (Part 4, PDF p.50); p.315 (Part 5, PDF p.8); p.316 (Part 5, PDF p.9); p.317 (Part 5, PDF p.10); p.318 (Part 5, PDF p.11); p.338 (Part 5, PDF p.31); p.345 (Part 5, PDF p.38); Appx B p.354 (Part 5, PDF p.47).

### M: "BM-numbers": the 1120-1151 range
- Category: Morse/binary
- Rules: Every integer from 1120 to 1151 begins in binary with 100011 = Morse **BM** (Berta Margoulies). The remaining 5 bits are read as a number or as Morse. Penn extends this to larger numbers that begin with 100011.
- Examples:
  - 1120 = 1000 11 00000: BM plus five zeros.
  - 1140 = 1000 11 10100 = **BM 20**, read as the "benchmark 20", i.e. the purse site at the apex of a 20° angle.
  - 1146 = BM 26 (26 = Z).
  - 1130 = 1000 11 01010 = BM "end of message".
  - 1151 = 1000 11 11111 = "BM zero" (Morse zero = 11111), or BM 31 (BM was 31 when she married), or a "family portrait" BM, Z, ERO (ERO = Eugene Robert O'Hare).
  - 11:34 + 11:46 = 2280 → "Morse 1000 11 101000, BM 80". That is as printed, but binary 101000 = 40. BM was about to begin her 80th year.
  - In the chess game the white queen moves only on squares with times 1120-1151.
  - BM 130 = 1000 11 10000010 = decimal 9090 = "NINE O x2".
- Claims: the calls identify Berta Margoulies and the benchmark-coded burial geometry.
- Citations: T17 p.233 (Part 4, PDF p.4); p.273 (Part 4, PDF p.44); p.283 (Part 4, PDF p.54); p.315 (Part 5, PDF p.8); p.316 (Part 5, PDF p.9); p.318 (Part 5, PDF p.11); p.342 (Part 5, PDF p.35); Appx B p.354 (Part 5, PDF p.47).

### M: Morse word + binary-numeral tail, and composite numbers as "Zodiac sentences"
- Category: Morse/binary
- Rules: A bit string is split into Morse letters followed by a binary numeral. Or two numbers are concatenated in binary and read as one string. Penn's sentence grammar: the subject "I" = 00 (two dits or two rings), the verb "am/is" is understood, and the predicate is a word or number. A trailing 0 after a letter is a "two-multiplier", because appending 0 doubles a binary number. A final 1 = "#1".
- Examples:
  - 104 = 1101000 and 157 = 10011101 (two call intervals) concatenated → 11 0 10 00 10 0 111 0 1 = **ME NINE O x2 #1**. Here BM 130 = NINE O x2, and "#1" = the first of two BM 130s on the map, in downtown Concord.
  - 11:01 → 10001001101 → 10 00 10 0 | 1101 = **NINE 13**. NINE may stand for "I", hence "I am thirteen". Alternatively, binary 913 = 1110010001 → 111 0010 00 1 = **OF IT**, giving "Thirty before the end OF IT".
  - "BER (am) I": THREE and BUS are both redivisions of BER + I.
  - 140 = 1000 11 00 = "BM [am] I".
  - "I (am) 232", the predicate of 00 + 232.
  - TITWILLO = "TITAN (am) I NINE O".
  - "Z (is) OH", "Z (is) HOH" (see the WIZARD method).
- Citations: T17 p.233 (Part 4, PDF p.4); p.309 (Part 5, PDF p.2); p.323 (Part 5, PDF p.16); Appx B pp.351, 354 (Part 5, PDF pp.44, 47).

### M: Number of rings read as Morse dits
- Category: Morse/binary
- Rules: Each ring counts as one dit (0), so a call or group of n rings is a string of n dits.
- Examples:
  - 2 rings = 00 = **I**, the subject of the sentence. This opens the 18 Dec 1984 series and the 12:04 call ("I TITAN 4").
  - 3 rings = 000 = **S**. The doubled 1238 was divided into three calls "because three rings is the same thing as three O's, 000, Morse letter S". The superfluous S of BUSS = three rings (three calls at 1:00, 1:03, 1:06 on 21 May = 5-20 = BUS).
  - 5 rings: "ring five", i.e. UTM line superscript 47 05. Five calls on 3 Aug 1984 answer Penn's Olympic five-rings card.
  - 7 rings = Circle Seven = "murder".
  - Two rings on Goose Pond = the OO of GOOSE. One ring at 1:27 = the O of CONCORD.
- Citations: T17 p.231 (Part 4, PDF p.2); p.234 (Part 4, PDF p.5); p.239 (Part 4, PDF p.10); p.240 (Part 4, PDF p.11); p.279 (Part 4, PDF p.50); p.280 (Part 4, PDF p.51); p.310 (Part 5, PDF p.3).

### M: Intervals (minutes) between consecutive calls
- Category: numerology/arithmetic + Morse/binary
- Rules: Compute the minutes between successive calls in a series. Read the intervals as Morse-binary words, multiples of 17, squares, Julian-type numbers, or digit strings. Concatenate them, or tabulate them as TIME / INTERVAL / ORDER / QUANTITY.
- Examples:
  - 5 Dec 1984, calls at 11:59, 12:38, 2:41: intervals 39 and 123, "The missing number is π (39π = 123)". Alternatively "1-2-3" = "count".
  - 14 Dec: calls at 11:26, 12:00, 4:47. 34 = 2x17; 287 = 1000 11 111 = **BMO** ("Berta Margoulies O'Hare").
  - 18 Dec: calls at 10:01, 10:01, 10:03, 10:16, 11:17, 3:04. The two rings are I; the intervals 2 and 13 give 213 (Julian date of the cryptogram's publication, = BM); 11:17 is the 11x17 paper size; 3:04 = TIMES. "213 and 11 x 17 is the message."
  - 7 Jan 1985 table (page image): TIME 10:03 10:15 10:16 11:03 11:50 3:06 3:21; INTERVAL 12, 1, 47, 47, 196, 15; ORDER #1, #2, #3, #4; QUANTITY 12, X, 14², 15. Here 1+47+47 = 95 = Morse TWO; 196 = 14²; the missing 13² = 169 → 16 = P, 9 = I → "The missing number is π."
  - 12:35 → 1:43 = 68 = NINE = 4x17. 10:51 → 12:12 = 81 = 9². 9:25 → 9:55 → 11:32 gives 30 and 97 (9-7, Berta's birthday), total 127 (MOM). 11:40 → 12:43 = 63 = 9x7. 12:07 → 4:21 = 254 = 2x127. The book's text says "12:07 and 2:41", but the grid and the 254 figure require 4:21.
  - 9:56 → 10:45 = 49 and 10:08 → 11:46 = 98: "three forty-nines". 30 + 98 = 128.
  - 10:45 → 2:37 = 232 = BERTA paraphrase (4x58).
  - 9:12 → 2:19 = 307, half = 153.5, cf. the 1:53 call (the text OCR reads "153.7"; the figure says 153.5).
  - 3:16 → 4:00 = 44 (see the reflex-angle method).
  - 11:40 → 12:51 → 2:22 → 2:26 gives 71, 91, 4. 91 + 4 = 95 = TWO. The 71st meridian, I-95 and Hwy 107 lie on the Lynn map.
- Citations: T17 p.231 (Part 4, PDF p.2); p.234 (Part 4, PDF p.5); p.237 (Part 4, PDF p.8); p.239 (Part 4, PDF p.10); p.240 (Part 4, PDF p.11); p.272 (Part 4, PDF p.43); p.273 (Part 4, PDF p.44); p.283 (Part 4, PDF p.54); p.308 (Part 5, PDF p.1); figure p.[232] (Part 4, PDF p.3).

### M: Quotients and ratios read as decimal digit strings
- Category: numerology/arithmetic
- Rules: Divide one interval or count by another and read the digits of the decimal result.
- Examples:
  - 50/106 = 0.47 = "ring 47", referring to UTM line 47 05.
  - 225/37 = 6.08 → "Six is ME, 0 is O, 8 is H. ME OH." Here 6 = binary 110 = ME, 0 = O (circle), 8 = H by alphabet position.
  - 37/27 = "1.38" (Penn). The true value is 1.370. He reads it as Intersection 138 near Fenn School.
  - 19/50 = 0.38: in the 340 cipher, characters #174 and 175 begin 19/50 of the way between crossed circles #155 and #205.
  - 278/259 = 1.07: characters above and below the dividing line in the 340 cover letter → Highway 107.

### M: Sums of clock times, and differences between those sums
- Category: numerology/arithmetic
- Rules: Add clock times as integers. Pairs may sit on a calendar-grid diagonal. Read the sums, or the differences between successive sums, as Morse-binary words or as doubles.
- Examples:
  - 1101 + 1116 = 2217; 1142 + 1143 = 2285; 2 x 1146 = 2292. The differences are 68 = 10 00 10 0 = NINE and 7 = 111 = O, giving "NINE O", shorthand for NINE O x2 (BM 130).
  - The answering-service calls 8:24 + 8:25 + 8:27 = 2476 = 2 x 1238, "divided into three parts" (= S).
  - The five calls of 2 Aug 1984 "add up to 3744 ... 117 x 32, or 117 followed by five zeros (rings): 1110101 00000". The five times printed in the figure (9:04, 11:40, 12:51, 2:22, 2:26) actually sum to 3743.
  - 10:17 + 10:21 = 2038 → "thirty before THE END" (2068 - 2038 = 30).
  - 11:34 + 11:46 = 2280 = "BM 80".
- Citations: T17 p.233 (Part 4, PDF p.4); p.240 (Part 4, PDF p.11); p.280 (Part 4, PDF p.51); p.309 (Part 5, PDF p.2); p.315 (Part 5, PDF p.8).

### M: Rebus "X before Y" = Y - X (and the variant "X left of Y")
- Category: word-play/pun + arithmetic
- Rules: A number written before another means subtract it: "12 before 50" = 50 - 12. Penn also reads a clock time as "N minutes before the hour". He applies the same rule to words ("ten before 88"). The variant "62 left of 66" counts days left in a year.
- Examples:
  - 12:50 = 50 - 12 = **38**. 2:52 = "eight before three" → 83, the bookend of 38.
  - 8:38 = "22 before 9" (figure caption).
  - 3:20 on 7 Aug then 10:37 on 8 Aug: 1037 - 320 = **717**.
  - In the chess game, 2:04 (= 12x17) then 10:37 (= 61x17): "Twelve before 61 is 61 minus twelve, or 49."
  - "4-TEEN": TEEN = 1 0 0 10 = binary 18, so 4 before 18 = 14. The same result arrives "two different ways".
  - "103 before MIKADO" → MIKE.
  - "I have killed ten people" → ten before PEOPLE (= 88) → 78 (see the victim-claim method).
  - 6266 → "62 left of 66" = 30 Oct 1966, when 62 days were left in the year (the Riverside murder).
- Citations: T17 p.230 (Part 4, PDF p.1); p.234 (Part 4, PDF p.5); p.237 (Part 4, PDF p.8); p.[238] fig. (Part 4, PDF p.9); p.272 (Part 4, PDF p.43); p.280 (Part 4, PDF p.51); p.327 (Part 5, PDF p.20); Appx D pp.361-362 (Part 5, PDF pp.54-55); Appx E p.369 (Part 5, PDF p.62).

### M: Mirror-image numbers ("bookends"), and reading from the opposite side of the table
- Category: numerology/arithmetic + grid/geometry
- Rules: A pair of digit- or letter-reversed items (123/321, BM/MB, BY/YB) "brackets" a structure, and the content lies between them or at their midpoint. Penn also reads a number upside down or reversed "from his perspective", as if O'Hare sat across the table looking at the calendar grid.
- Examples:
  - Calls at 1:23 a.m. and 3:21 form vertical bookends on the grid. 321 = 3 x 107 (Hwy 107); BM 123 is on the Concord map.
  - 9:12 and 2:19 are vertical bookends.
  - 38 ... 83 (12:50 ... 2:52).
  - 340 cipher: BM ... MB bracket crossed circle #3 = character #117. BY ... YB in lines 10-11 (characters #168 and #181) bracket an even run; the middle pair #174 + #175 = **349**. 349 is the mirror of 943 = binary "117 O" (Riverside). The figure is on PDF p.7.
  - Confession Letter by-line "BY____" + "SHE WAS YOUNG AND BEAUTIFUL": B is the 2nd letter and Y the next-to-last, so "BY...Y...B", with 15 letters between the B's → 15 years. 15 years = 5475 + 4 leap days = 5479 days, which read backwards is 9745 (Berta's 38th birthday, TIMES 17).
  - A 2:30 a.m. call read backwards is 032 = 2^5.
  - Seen from the opposite side, 349 → 943.
  - 451 in binary is the mirror of MHO's bits (Appx E).
  - UE disguised as FV/VF (mirror pairs) at 340-cipher characters 89 and 98, which are also mirror numbers.
- Citations: T17 p.234 (Part 4, PDF p.5); p.[236] (Part 4, PDF p.7); p.237 (Part 4, PDF p.8); p.[238] (Part 4, PDF p.9); p.239 (Part 4, PDF p.10); p.241 (Part 4, PDF p.12); p.243 (Part 4, PDF p.14); p.274 (Part 4, PDF p.45); Appx B p.351 (Part 5, PDF p.44); Appx E p.369 (Part 5, PDF p.62).

### M: Binary "degree sign": append Morse O (111) to a number
- Category: Morse/binary
- Rules: To write N°, write N in binary and append 111 (Morse O, standing in for the superscript circle).
- Examples: 117 = 1110101, and with O appended 1110101111 = **943**. "943...032 = 117 O...32" and "943...128 = 117 O...128" mirror O'Hare's SSN 117-32-2128. On the Riverside poem, "Someonell (9) find (4) her (3)" = 943, so 349 must be found ("Was it Fenn School?"). Binary 117 = binary 58 followed by 1: 111010|1.
- Claims: 117° is Riverside's longitude, and 349 marks the Webster crime.
- Citations: T17 p.[238] (Part 4, PDF p.9, figure); p.239 (Part 4, PDF p.10); p.243 (Part 4, PDF p.14); p.274 (Part 4, PDF p.45); Appx B p.354 (Part 5, PDF p.47).

### M: Calendar-grid geometry of the call log
- Category: grid/geometry + calendar/date
- Rules: Plot every call on a weekly calendar grid (columns Sun-Sat, rows = weeks). Read structures along rows, columns and diagonals: progressions, bookends, intersections of a diagonal with a vertical (which Penn says mirror the map constructions), letter shapes, and right triangles whose legs are minute differences.
- Examples:
  - The diagonal pairs 11:01/11:16, 11:42/11:43 and 11:46 x2 give NINE O. It intersects the vertical ME NINE / ME NINE O x2 #1, "just as" the map's 12:28 azimuth intersected the Goose Pond line.
  - 1:49, 2:49, 3:49 form a diagonal progression, with 2:02 (MIKE) sandwiched in.
  - The four 11-o'clock entries form a letter **Y** on the grid. Morse Y = 1011 = binary 11, and the centre 11:22 = eleven and its double.
  - Right triangle: 11:20 → 11:32 horizontally (12 min) and 11:20 → 11:40 vertically (20 min). Hypotenuse = √(400 + 144) = √544, and 544 = 1000100000 = 10 00 10 0 000 = **NINES** = 32 x 17. "Her course takes her along the square root of NINES, or three squares."
  - The 4-1-7 diagonal ("four seventeens", "one seventeen", "seven seventeens") runs along a 3x3 "square of nine". 9² = 81, the year of the Webster murder.
  - The Wednesday column is the centre column of the grid, as 573 is the centre column of the magic square.
- Citations: T17 pp.[232]-233 (Part 4, PDF pp.3-4); p.239 (Part 4, PDF p.10); p.273 (Part 4, PDF p.44); p.[271] fig. (Part 4, PDF p.42); p.280 (Part 4, PDF p.51); p.342 (Part 5, PDF p.35).

### M: Chess game encoded on the calendar grid
- Category: grid/geometry (game)
- Rules: Treat the 8-week grid as a chessboard. It has 8 columns because Penn adds a phantom rook's column "Noneday" before Sunday, balanced by the blank Saturdays. The week of 3-9 Feb 1985 is White's home row. Pieces are identified by their numbers, and moves are read as knight's moves (L-shapes), diagonals and so on. Penn plays Black and never moves.
- Examples:
  - 10:00 = 1000 = Morse B, so the 10:49 and 10:51 calls are the Bishops.
  - Tuesday's 3:31 x Wednesday's 12:18. Here 1218 = 1 00 11 0 000 10 = TIMES 2, so 331 TIMES 2 = 662 = 1010010110 = 101 00 10 110 = **KING**. 662 is too large for a clock face, so it was halved and given a multiplier.
  - The bishops' minutes are 17, 49, 51. The intervals 32 and 2, read right to left, are the powers of two of the SSN, in the same order.
  - Queen's knight: every square is marked 127 (MOM) or 254.
  - King's knight: 12:35 = TIMEX 1; 12:35 → 1:43 = 68 = NINE = 4x17; 2:04 = 12x17 and 10:37 = 61x17, with 61 - 12 = 49. He "holds my queen in peril (1:49)" and exits off the board via 2:02 (MIKE) to 3:49 (= 943 seen from White's side).
  - King's bishop: 10:51 → 12:12 = 81 = 9² (a diagonal across nine squares), then 1:25 = 5³, because the square is in "the fifth row, third column" once Noneday is counted.
  - Queen: always on 1120-1151 (BM); 9-7 interval pairs; 1122 = 66x17; the √544 = NINES hypotenuse.
  - The game as printed: 1) Q-Q2 2) QN-QB3 3) QN-Q5 4) Q-KB4 5) KN-K2 6) QN-QB7 (check) 7) KN-Q4 8) KB-Q3 9) KN-QB6 10) KB-KB5, "Black to escape checkmate in one. White to take Black queen -- to 3:49".
  - Figures: CHESS GAME, KNIGHTS' MOVES, QUEEN'S AND BISHOP'S MOVES, PDF pp.39, 41, 42.
- Claims: White queen = Berta, queen's knight = O'Hare's public "Jekyll" side (MOM), king's knight = his Hyde side (TIMES 17 / TIMEX / 68 / 49 = the Zodiac career). Black = Penn. "White sees everything as a mirror-image of what I see", citing the White Knight in *Through the Looking-Glass*. The game was Diane's joke prediction ("Mike would start playing chess with me over the telephone").
- Citations: T17 pp.[268]-274 (Part 4, PDF pp.39-45).

### M: Numerical series hidden in clock times (Fibonacci, consecutive integers, powers, squares)
- Category: numerology/arithmetic
- Rules: Read the digits of a clock time, or of a group of calls, as the start of a known integer sequence. A gap in the sequence is the "title" or the missing message.
- Examples:
  - 12:35 = 1, 2, 3, 5, the beginning of the Fibonacci series. Penn replied at 3:58 Eastern after "scheming for 23 minutes".
  - 12:48 = 1, 2, 4, 8 (powers of two); 1:00 = 10²; 1:21 = 11². The missing 9² = 81 gives the title "Square of Nine" (figure PDF p.52).

  - Penn's own reply to the 1:23 a.m. call was a call at 4:56 a.m. his time ("4-5-6" answering "1-2-3"). His reply on 10 Dec 1986 was at 3:45 a.m. Massachusetts time.
  - 3:45 = the "3-4-5 right triangle" (see the time-zone method).

### M: "Guess the missing number" puzzles
- Category: numerology/arithmetic
- Rules: Find a series with one member absent or implied. The absent member is the message, often π.
- Examples:
  - 12, X, 14², 15 → X = 13² = 169 → P(16) I(9) → π.
  - 39 and 123 → 39π = 123.
  - 391, from Appx E: Riverside → Lake Herman Road = 782 days = 2 x 391. Lake Herman Road → Blue Rock Springs (4 July) = 196 days, which is half of 391 = 195.5 rounded up ("in every place excepting the U.S. Army artillery"). So 391 is the missing number, and 391 = 23 x 17.
  - The postcard Penn sent O'Hare on 8 Apr 1985 posed "x + 10 = 138. x + 9 = 130. Solve for x." The answer is 128: the site was 10 mm from Intersection 138 and 68 mm (NINE) from BM 130.

### M: Social Security number decomposition (117-32-2128 → 117, 2^5, 2^7)
- Category: numerology/arithmetic
- Rules: Split O'Hare's SSN into 117 (Riverside's longitude), 32 = 2^5 and 2128 = 2 then 128 = 2^7. Look for these values, in this order or reversed, among the calls.

### M: Penn's TIMES 17 magic square, matched to the call log
