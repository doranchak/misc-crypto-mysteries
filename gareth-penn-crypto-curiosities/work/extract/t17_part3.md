## Methods

### M: Nine-multiple seat counts + Golden-Section split ("whatsit" table)
- Category: numerology/arithmetic; error-tagging
- Rules: In O'Hare's dissertation Table 5-1 (Harvard classrooms), take the four rows for the unnamed building "whatsit" (bldg 399). Read their seating capacities as a series; split the last term by the Golden Section fraction 0.6180339.
- Examples: whatsit rows (read from page images): room 00007 seats 81; room 00001 seats 72; room 00002 seats 39; room 00107 seats 24 (Table 5-1 pp.1-3). Penn: "a perfect series of nine-multiples. The last one, expressed by the sum of 39 and 24, is the product of the numerical elements of Berta Margoulies' birthday, 9-7" (81, 72, 63 = 9x9, 9x8, 9x7). "63 x 0.6180339... = 39" (exact: 38.94). Room numbers 7+1+2+107 = 117.
- Claims: seat counts fabricated to encode BM's birthday 9-7 and the Golden Section; room sum = 117 = Riverside; "tagged with error (the misplaced building name)".
- Citation: T17 p.152 (Part 3, PDF p.1); table pages + caption "THE ROOMS OF 'WHATSIT'" T17 p.153-156 (PDF p.2-5); recalled T17 p.175 (PDF p.24).

- Category: other (meta-rule)
