## Source 1: Mensa Bulletin, Jul/Aug 1985, "The Calculus of Evil" (by Gareth Penn)

Layout: scan 1 = cover; scan 2 = printed p.5 (title page, byline "Gareth Penn"); scan 3 = p.6; scan 4 = p.7;
(later scans listed in their sections). The whole article is Penn's own text (byline on p.5); editorial
matter is limited to the cover line and pull-quotes (e.g. p.5 pull-quote "The Zodiac claimed to be
identifying himself in some kind of code.").

Penn's stated premises (MB scan 2, p.5 col.3 and scan 3, p.6 col.1):
1. "whatever the Zodiac said, no matter how goofy-sounding, was true" (truth hidden "behind a Delphic
   smokescreen").
2. Because the author used "a rather advanced mathematical form (the radian)", he would "avail himself
   of a mathematical idiom of some sort"; Penn looked for "a means of translating linguistic expressions
   into number". Two came to mind: (a) A=1 ... Z=26; (b) Morse code read as binary.
3. "The Zodiac uses both methods. But where he uses a letter for its alphabetic quantity, that quantity
   is then readable in Morse Code." (scan 3, p.6 col.1). He also stresses "formulations that produce the
   same results in two different ways" (scan 3, p.6 col.2).

### M: Radian laid on Mount Diablo (map geometry)
- Category: map/geography, grid/geometry
- Rules: Take the June 1970 letter's suggestion ("something interesting" if a radian is placed on
  Mount Diablo). Put the apex of an angle of 1 radian (Penn: "a circle divided by 2pi") on the summit of
  Mount Diablo; lay one leg through one crime site and see where the other leg falls.
- Examples: apex on Mt. Diablo peak; one leg through Blue Rock Springs (Vallejo, 4 Jul 1969); the other
  leg "passes directly through Presidio Heights in San Francisco" (site of San Francisco, 11 Oct 1969).
  Penn: the Zodiac "had been constructing a gigantic geometric design on the map of the San Francisco Bay
  Area" with the crime sites as markers. He adds that Z "drew a clock face on a map, centered
  on Mount Diablo" (scan 3, p.6 col.2).
- Claims: the radian explains the choice of the San Francisco site; "an m.o., a mathematical one".
- Citation: Mensa Bulletin Jul/Aug 1985, scan page 2 (p.5), col.2-3. Penn says he learned of the radian
  letter "in late 1980", told police, and was briefly treated as a suspect ("the most I could be charged
  with was possession of a dictionary and protractor").

### M: Alphabet value A=1..Z=26 (gematria) combined with Morse binary
- Category: letter-number, Morse/binary
- Rules: A letter's alphabetic ordinal is to be compared with a Morse-binary reading of another letter
  group; two different encodings giving the same number = deliberate signal. Penn cites the ancient
  Greeks and Kabbalah as precedent and says Napa Sheriff's investigators had suspected A=1..Z=26 was the
  key to the misspellings "but never found a consistent system".
- Examples (scan 3, p.6 col.1-2):
  - Compare misspellings **FRY** ("kill rampage ... from Fry to Sun", cover letters of the July 1969
    cryptogram) and **MERY** ("mery Xmass", letter to Melvin Belli, Dec 1969). Both end in -RY, so the
    prefixes F- and ME- must be equivalent: ME in Morse = 11 0 = **110 = binary 6**; F = 6th letter.
    "In both cases, then, we have a form of the number six preceding the same group, -RY."
  - **-RY**: Morse R=010, Y=1011 -> **0101011**; "overlooking the leading zero" = binary **43**.
    Alphabetically R=18, Y=25, sum **43**. Same result two ways.
  - Resulting "sentence": **"ME 43"** = subject (first person) + predicate, with the verb "to be"
    omitted "as it is in the modern Slavic languages".
- Claims: "ME 43" is "a typical Zodiac sentence" (a self-description by number). [What 43 means is
  not stated on this page.]
- Citation: Mensa Bulletin Jul/Aug 1985, scan page 3 (p.6), cols.1-2.

### M: Morse code read as a binary number (dot=0, dash=1)
- Category: Morse/binary
- Rules: Write each letter in Morse, replace dot by 0 and dash by 1, concatenate (letter boundaries
  dropped), read as a base-2 integer. Leading zeros may be dropped ("overlooking the leading zero").
- Examples:
  - Vanity plate seen in California: "0001 011 (VW - zero is dot, one is dash)" (scan 3, p.6 col.1).
  - ME = 110 = 6; RY = 0101011 = 43 (above).
  - **TIME** = 1 00 11 0 = binary **38** (scan 3, p.6 col.3).
  - **THREE** = 1 0000 010 0 0 and **BUS** = 1000 001 000: same digit string, "base-two **520**"
    (scan 3, p.6 col.3).
  - **THAE** (in the 1969 three-part cryptogram, Harden solution, Examiner portion, line 14
    "RTOFITIATHAEWHENI") = 1 0000 01 0 = **130** ("Highlighted: THAE becomes 130 binary", Fig. 1 caption).
  - **TWO** = 1 011 111 = 1011111 = **95** (scan 4, p.7 col.3; see Exorcist letter method).
- Citation: Mensa Bulletin Jul/Aug 1985, scan pages 3-4 (pp.6-7).

### M: "Redivision" of a Morse bit string into other letters/numbers
- Category: Morse/binary
- Rules: Take the bit string of one Morse word; re-cut it at different boundaries into other Morse
  letters, or into a binary number plus a Morse letter.
- Examples:
  - THREE (1 0000 010 0 0) <-> BUS (1000 001 000): "redivisions of the same sequence of digits
    (base-two 520)" (scan 3, p.6 col.3). Penn links this to the Zodiac's repeated bus threats
    (mailings 4-8) and to the July 1969 mailings' emphasis on THREE.
  - Third redivision: **1000001000 -> 10000010 | 00 = "130" followed by Morse I (00)**, which "functions
    here as the subject of a two-part sentence, 'I (am) 130.'" (scan 4, p.7 col.1).
- Claims: the Zodiac identifies himself as 130.
- Citation: Mensa Bulletin Jul/Aug 1985, scan page 3 (p.6) col.3 and scan page 4 (p.7) col.1.

### M: Recurring "three" (mailing counts, postage, postmarks)
- Category: numerology/arithmetic, calendar/date
- Rules: Count copies, recipients, parts, stamps, binder rings, and read postmark dates as day-of-year.
- Examples (scan 3, p.6 col.3): first Riverside letter, 2 copies, postmarked **29 Nov 1966 = day 333**
  of the year ("In almost all cases, he used dates to express significant numbers when read as the day of
  the year"); second Riverside letter in 3 copies, on three-ring binder paper, franked with three-cent
  stamps; the cryptogram in three parts. Then the bus threats -> THREE/BUS redivision.
- Claims: "pulled out all the stops in his efforts to underscore the number three".
- Citation: Mensa Bulletin Jul/Aug 1985, scan page 3 (p.6) col.3.

### M: Day-of-year reading of dates/postmarks
- Category: calendar/date
- Rules: Convert a date (usually a postmark) to its ordinal day in the year; treat that as the message.
- Examples: 29 Nov 1966 -> 333 (non-leap year: 304 + 29). [Other instances appear later in the article.]
- Citation: Mensa Bulletin Jul/Aug 1985, scan page 3 (p.6) col.3.

### M: The number 38 = TIME (letter counts, street-block numbers)
- Category: letter-number, numerology/arithmetic, word-play/pun
- Rules: Morse TIME = 100110 = 38. Then count letters in plaintext and find 38s; read street block
  numbers; collect "time" puns (Timex watch left at a scene; USPS clock-face stickers "Mail early in the
  day"; clock face drawn on map centered on Mt. Diablo; *Chronicle* < Greek *chronos*; correspondents
  *Vallejo Times-Herald*, *Los Angeles Times*).
- Examples:
  - "I like killing people because it is so much fun" = **38 letters**; the first spelling error, the
    double R of "forrest", occurs **38 letters later** (scan 3, p.6 col.2).
  - San Francisco, 11 Oct 1969: the cab was directed to Maple & Washington, then Cherry & Washington; the
    block between = the **3800 block**; another scene detail Penn also reads as 38 (scan 3, p.6 col.3).
  - Exorcist letter: 76 mm over 38 mm (see below); "yet another repetition of the number 38, which is a
    running theme in the literature" (scan 4, p.7 col.3).
- Citation: Mensa Bulletin Jul/Aug 1985, scan pages 3-4 (pp.6-7).

### M: 390:18 = pi proportion in the 408-cryptogram (letter count split)
- Category: numerology/arithmetic
- Rules: Split the 408-symbol cryptogram's solution into the English part and the trailing "anagram"
  (EBEORIETEMETHHPITI). Count letters; compare the ratio with the integer : fraction parts of pi.
- Examples: English = **390** letters, anagram = **18**. 390 = 130 x 3; 18 = 130 x 0.14159...; so the
  text division "reproduces the number **130pi**" (scan 3, p.6 col.3). (Penn writes the anagram as
  "EBEORIETEMETHHPITI".) 24 lines x 17 columns, mailed in three parts of 8 lines each
  (Times-Herald / Examiner / Chronicle).
- Claims: 130 again (cf. THAE = 130, "I am 130").
- Citation: Mensa Bulletin Jul/Aug 1985, scan page 3 (p.6) cols.2-3, and Fig. 1 (scan 3, top).

### M: Z340 "digital" key: nine-multiples symbol class -> 1, else 0
- Category: cipher (classical / binary), grid/geometry
- Rules (scan 4, p.7 cols.1-2): The 340 is not a substitution cipher but "digital". Since it has more
  than two symbols, a key assigns 0/1 to symbol classes. "The crossed circle - Z.'s signature - proved
  to be that key. It appears nine times" [so the key number is nine; Penn does not spell out the step
  further]. He "hypothesized three types of symbols belonging to the nine-multiples class", all set = 1:
  - geometric forms: circle, square, triangle ("Triangle is a geometric form having a sum of angles
    divisible by nine (20x9)");
  - letter shapes: C = semicircle ("180 deg, also 20x9"), L = right angle, X = cross;
  - alphabetical order divisible by 9: I = 9, R = 18.
  All others = 0. "I left the six dots completely alone." Read row by row (20 rows x 17).
- Supporting observation: the crossed circle "appears nine times. The last time it is ninth from the
  end; immediately following it is ... a distortion of the word ZODIAC, then two extraneous characters.
  The distortions are: triangle (delta) for D, K for C (phonetic equivalents), and inversion of A and I"
  (i.e. the cipher's row 20 ends: crossed-circle, Z O (triangle) A I K, + 2 extra symbols).
- Penn's binary translation (Fig. 2b, scan 4, transcribed from the image; "." = the six dots left as is):

- Examples / results ("Highlighted (cipher and binary translation), in order: The decimal fraction of
  pi; 130; 35; crossed circle marks #117; 22 before 9" - Fig. 2 caption):
  - First 13 digits **0010010000111** = the binary fraction of pi to 13 places (pi = 11.0010010000111111...).

    cell 49 (row 3 col 15) and 100011 (=35) starts at cell 73 (row 5 col 5), both crossed-circle cells
    in the cipher image [cell positions inferred by me from the figure; Penn gives only the numbers].
  - Third crossed circle = the **117th** character (row 7 col 15).
  - Last nine characters (cells 332-340, starting at the final crossed circle) **101101001** read as
    10110 | 1001 = **"22 before 9"**; Penn reads this as the two numbers of the first two Bay Area attacks
    (Lake Herman Road, 20 Dec 1968; Blue Rock Springs, 4 Jul 1969), "or, read as clock time, giving
    **8:38**" (22 minutes before 9).
- Claims: 130, 35, 117 "all appear repeatedly in the Zodiac literature"; the cipher is a numeric message.
- Citation: Mensa Bulletin Jul/Aug 1985, scan page 4 (p.7), Fig. 2a/2b and cols.1-2.

### M: "TIMES 17": rectangular format = multiplication
- Category: grid/geometry, numerology/arithmetic, word-play/pun
- Rules: A message written in an R x C rectangle means "R times C"; a lone number at the foot of a
  letter to the *Times* means "Times x N".
- Examples: 408 cryptogram = **24 x 17**; Z340 = **20 x 17**; 1971 letter to the *Los Angeles Times*
  with the footnote "17" (the Chronicle read it as a claimed tally; Penn: "he had said no such thing")
  = **Los Angeles Times 17**. "The rectangular format implies multiplication ... The phrase **TIMES 17**
  is common to all three." Parallel: the Drake-Sagan (Arecibo) radio message of Nov 1974, whose
  length, a product of two primes, suggests its rectangular format (footnote: *Icarus* 26, pp.462-466
  (1975); *Scientific American*, May 1975, pp.80-89).
- Claims: origin of the phrase/title "Times 17".
- Citation: Mensa Bulletin Jul/Aug 1985, scan page 4 (p.7) col.2 and footnote.

### M: Linear measurement of i-dot distances (Exorcist letter "tutorial")
- Category: typography/handwriting, grid/geometry, Morse/binary
- Rules: Measure the physical document and the distances (mm) between the dots of misplaced/misspelled
  i's, "center to center", taken "in syntactic order"; read the intervals as numbers; check them by
  Morse-binary and arithmetic. Credit: "Dr. Leonti Thompson with the brilliant suggestion that the i-dots
  form a geometric design." "One of the ways in which Z. expresses number is as linear measurement."
- Examples (scan 4, p.7 col.3): Exorcist letter (mailed to the *Chronicle* late Jan 1974; "most [of the
  misspellings] involve misplacement of the letter I"); sheet cut to **24 cm x 17 cm** (again 24 x 17);
  i-dot intervals **76 mm, 38 mm, 95 mm**; the 76 mm line lies **above** the 38 mm line. 95 = binary
  1011111 = Morse 1 011 111 = **TWO**; and 76 "over" 38 = 2 = TWO. "The statement is true, both
  mathematically and orthographically" - a "tutorial" on how to read the document (cf. Arecibo).
- Citation: Mensa Bulletin Jul/Aug 1985, scan page 4 (p.7) col.3.

### M: Equal i-dot spacing as "N TIMES 17" (titwillo)
- Category: typography/handwriting, numerology/arithmetic
- Rules: Count repetitions of a word and measure i-dot spacing inside each; read as "count TIMES spacing".
- Examples: Exorcist letter (late Jan 1974): in each of the three repetitions of "titwillo" the i-dots
  are "exactly 17mm apart, center to center. Try reading it as **THREE TIMES 17**". Fig. 3 Detail 2
  ("Z's loose-looking scrawl hides amazing precision") brackets: 17mm over each "titwillo"; 76mm between
  i-dots on the line "I saw + think 'The Exorcist'"; 95mm and 38mm on the "saterical comidy" line
  [exact endpoints of the 95/38 brackets read from the figure; approximate].
- Citation: Mensa Bulletin Jul/Aug 1985, scan page 5 (p.8) col.1 and Fig. 3 Detail 2.

### M: Drawn figures read as protractor angles (Exorcist letter)
- Category: grid/geometry
- Rules: Treat the line figures drawn at the foot of a letter as angles; measure with a protractor;
  read the degree values as "familiar" numbers.
- Examples (Fig. 3 Detail 1, "The geometric figures viewed as angles"): leftmost annotated angle
  subtends **130 deg**; to its right it "joins legs with a **100 deg** angle", as does a third angle of
  **35 deg** (Penn: "I suppose that sounds a little risque"). These "translate into a given name, a
  monogram and a nickname - which belong to a married couple I believe are the killer's parents".
  (Decoded in Penn's 20 Aug 1985 letter, below: 130 = woman's name, 35 = her monogram, 100 = husband's
  name.)
- Citation: Mensa Bulletin Jul/Aug 1985, scan page 5 (p.8) col.1 and Fig. 3 Detail 1.

### M: Event-timing correlation (mailings vs. outside calendar)
- Category: calendar/date
- Rules: Align the dates of the Zodiac's bursts of activity with the meeting dates of an outside body.
- Examples: the Bay Area debut coincided with a developer preparing a construction-permit application
  at Incline [Village], Nevada (Lake Tahoe); three later bursts "over the next year and a half" coincide
  with planning-commission meetings on the permit; the project was killed and the Zodiac fell silent
  (1971-74). The last pre-silence mailing showed a picture of a housing project at Lake Tahoe
  (scan 4, p.7 col.3). Penn: "I cannot spell out the specifics".
- Claims: corroborates the identification "against very long odds".
- Citation: Mensa Bulletin Jul/Aug 1985, scan page 5 (p.8) cols.2-3.

### M: Map triangle angles = latitude/longitude numbers (122 deg, 38 deg)
- Category: map/geography, grid/geometry
- Rules: Connect three sites on a map; measure the triangle's angles; match them to a graticule
  intersection (meridian, parallel) relevant to an earlier message.
- Examples: the "bomb" said to be buried near Mt. Diablo (same letter as the radian, June 1970) ->
  Concord Naval Weapons Station, "unremarkable ... except for an imaginary line placed there by the
  U.S. Geological Survey: the intersection of the **122nd Meridian** with the **38th Parallel**." Then a
  Boston-area case (Nov 1981, last seen at Logan Airport): triangle connecting the airline terminal with
  the two sites where the person's belongings were found has angles of **122 deg** and **38 deg**.
  Penn adds the suspect was 38 years old then.
- Citation: Mensa Bulletin Jul/Aug 1985, scan page 5 (p.8) col.3.

## Source 1b: Penn's typed follow-up "Dear Correspondents" letter, 20 Aug 1985, with "PLEASE IDENTIFY THE ZODIAC." (Mensa scan pages 7-14)

Scans 7-14 are not the magazine: each is one landscape typed sheet by Penn, numbered 1-8 at top centre (scan N = sheet p.N-6), set in two columns. Scan 7 left col. = cover letter

(answers to Mensa readers who wrote in). Penn says the MB article was "considerably shorter than my copy"

### M: Digital names (number = name), monogram as number, surname = pi fraction reversed
- Category: Morse/binary, letter-number
- Rules: A person's name is expressed as the Morse-binary number of its letters ("digital name"); a
  monogram likewise; a surname may be spelled backwards in Morse bits.
- Examples (scan 7 right, sheet p.1, right col.):
  - "**130** is the digital name of a woman. Her monogram is given digitally as **35**. Her husband's name
    is **100**." [Penn does not spell the names here; 130 = 10000010, 35 = 100011, 100 = 1100100.]
  - "The surname which they share (with their son, too) is spelled, in reverse, by the fraction of pi to
    thirteen places in binary notation": pi fraction **0010010000111** = Morse **E R A H O**
    (0 010 01 0000 111) = O'HARE reversed [the name is my decoding; Penn withholds it].
  - Exorcist letter (1974) shows a 130-deg angle joined to a 100-deg angle: "130" and "100" were
    observing their **37th anniversary** ("see the box-score, 'Me 37,' at the foot of this document").
- Citation: Mensa scan 7 right (sheet p.1, right col.).

### M: Morse word + binary number concatenated, read as a date (TIMES 17 = 9-7-45)
- Category: Morse/binary, calendar/date
- Rules: Concatenate the Morse bits of a word with the plain binary of a number; convert to decimal;
  split the decimal digits as M-D-YY.
- Examples: **TIMES 17** = TIMES (1 00 11 0 000) + 17 (10001) = **10011000010001** = base-ten **9745** =
  **9-7-45** (7 Sep 1945): "'130' celebrated her 38th birthday on that date".
- Citation: Mensa scan 7 right (sheet p.1, right col.).

### M: Total letter count of a document -> binary -> Morse redivision (1623 = MIKE O)
- Category: letter-number, Morse/binary
- Rules: Count all letters in a document; write the count in base 2; cut into Morse letters.
- Examples: the first letter after Riverside, 30 Oct 1966, the typewritten "THE CONFESSION" (with the
  literal unfinished by-line "BY ____") is "composed in **1623** letters"; 1623 = **11001010111** =
  "MIKE O (11 00 101 0 111)". Penn: Z. "was, as usual, being truthful when he put this title on the
  letter" [i.e. a confession whose signature is its letter count; my gloss].
- Citation: Mensa scan 7 right (sheet p.1, right col.).

### M: Implied word (MIKADO) found as Morse bits in the 340 binary; matching "103" offset in mm
- Category: Morse/binary, typography/handwriting
- Rules: A quotation from a work implies its title; the title's Morse string is then searched for in the
  digital Z340, and its offset compared with a mm distance in another letter.
- Examples: letters of 7-27-70 and 1-29-74 quote Gilbert & Sullivan's *The Mikado* without naming it.
  MIKADO in Morse = 11 00 101 01 100 111 (a "15-digit sequence"), found in the 340 binary "preceded by
  **103** digits". [Check against my transcription: cells 104-119 read 110010101100.111, i.e. MIKADO
  if the dot at cell 116 is skipped; cell 117 is Penn's "#117" crossed circle.] In the Exorcist letter
  the line above the Mikado quote has the salutation "Signed, yours truley:"; "the distance between the
  dot of 'Signed' and the upper dot of the colon is **103mm**". "In both cases, we have 103 somethings
  preceding either the word MIKADO or a quotation from the operetta."
- Citation: Mensa scan 7 right (sheet p.1, right col.).

### M: Mathematical rebus: "over" = division, "before" = subtraction
- Category: numerology/arithmetic, word-play/pun
- Rules: Spatial/verbal relations between numbers are arithmetic operators: one number written over
  another = division (76 over 38 = 2 = TWO); one expression *before* another = subtraction (cf. "22
  before 9" = 8:38).
- Citation: Mensa scan 7 right (sheet p.1, right col., last para., continued on scan 8).

### M: Subtraction rebus "103 before MIKADO" = MIKE (Roman-numeral logic)
- Category: Morse/binary, numerology/arithmetic, word-play/pun
- Rules: "one expression *before* another implies subtraction. This follows the logic of Roman
  numerals: IV is one before five". Write the word in Morse bits, locate the binary of the number as a
  substring, delete it; read the remainder as Morse.
- Examples: two documents five years apart both give "103 before MIKADO" (103 cells before MIKADO in
  the Z340 binary; 103 mm before the *Mikado* quote in the Exorcist letter). MIKADO = 11 00 101 0**1 100
  111** (underlined part = 1100111 = binary **103**); remove it -> **11 00 101 0 = MIKE**.
- Claims: the Zodiac's first name is MIKE (cf. 1623 = MIKE O).
- Citation: Mensa scan 8 left (sheet p.2, left col.), first paragraph.

### M: Hidden "angular artifact" in a published photograph (doctored shadow)
- Category: grid/geometry, typography/handwriting (visual)

### M: "Alphabetic-symbols-only" Morse transcription of a symbol cipher
- Category: cipher (classical), Morse/binary
- Rules: Take the cipher symbols in order. Symbols that are Roman letters (including rotated/reversed
  forms Penn reads as letters) are written as Morse bits; all non-alphabetic symbols ("geometric forms,
  Greek letters, etc.") are dropped (printed as "-"); the crossed circle is kept as a marker "*". Then
  remove the dashes, concatenate, and redivide into binary numbers.
- Example (32-character "Mt. Diablo" cipher, first 25 symbols "through cipher symbol H"):
  alphabetic symbols **CJIOKAMFORTGX FDVJH**; Morse (as printed, "-" = omitted symbols):
  `1010-011100-111101-01110010-11101011101001-0010100000101110000`
  Redivided (dashes removed):
  `10100 111001 111010 1110010 1110101 11010010010 10000010 1110000`
  = **20, 57, 58, 114, 117, 1682, 130, 112** (I verified the concatenation and all conversions).
- Penn's readings (scan 2, p.4):
  - 130 = the "Zodiac commonplace" (woman's given name in Morse, per MB); "**112** is an abbreviated
    form of that person's surname" [112 = 1110000 = Morse O H; my decoding]. No comment on 20 or 1682.
  - **57 ... 117** is "a progression and a sub-progression": 114 = (57 x 2) + 0; 117 = (58 x 2) + 1;
    57, 58 is one progression; 0, 1 another. 57 and 58 "bound the value of the radian, which is
    57 deg 17' 44.80625"" (radian = 180/pi). Editor's note (p.10): "The 0 and 1 bound the decimal part
    (.29) of the radian value."
  - **117** = longitude of Riverside, CA (Riverside, 30 Oct 1966; the link to Riverside was first
    published by Paul Avery in the *Chronicle*, 16-17 Nov 1970, five months after this cipher).
  - The accompanying **Phillips 66** road map contributes **66** = the year of the Riverside crime:
    "The 117 gives the location in space; 66 gives the location in time." The "bomb" = a "bombshell":
    "The explosion ... is a publicistic one."
- Citation: The Cryptogram Nov-Dec 1986, scan page 1 (p.3) and scan page 2 (p.4).

### M: Count the digits; then "2 NINE" before the crossed circle (= 29)
- Category: Morse/binary, numerology/arithmetic
- Rules: The number of binary digits in a passage is itself meaningful; the following symbols are
  redivided into a binary number plus a Morse word.
- Examples: the series through symbol No. 25 (H) "is written in **57 digits**". Then symbols Nos. 26-28
  **C E L** = 1010 0 0100, followed by the crossed circle (No. 29). Redivided: **10 | 10 00 10 0 | ***
  = binary **2** + Morse **N I N E** -> "**2 NINE**", followed by the crossed circle, "character
  **29**". Radian in decimal = 57.**29**57795131 deg (Penn underlines "29"): 57 digits then "29" two
  ways (as 2 NINE and as position 29).
- Claims: matches the July 1970 postscript "The Mt. Diablo Code concerns Radians & # inches along the
  radians" (quoted by Penn), so the cipher and map "give the location of a hidden bomb" as claimed.
- Citation: The Cryptogram Nov-Dec 1986, scan page 2 (p.4).

### M: Reverse the whole digit string (13-character cipher read backwards)
- Category: Morse/binary, cipher (classical)
- Rules: Transcribe as above, then write "the same sequence of digits ... in reverse" and redivide.
- Example (13-character cipher, April 1970): symbol sequence given as **AEN*-K-M-NAM**; digital:
  ```
  A  E  N  *  K   M  N  A  M
  01 0  10 *  101 11 10 01 11
  ```
  Reversed (p.10): **111001 | 11101 | * | 01010** = **57 | 29 | (crossed circle) | "the end"**.
  "The group 01010 is standard Morse telegrapher's usage for 'end of message'" (= the "xxx" Penn types
  at the end of his article). "This cipher expresses exactly the same thing as the 32-character
  cipher, but in simpler form: the integer of the radian (57), is followed by the first two digits of
  the radian-fraction (.29), and then the crossed circle." The two ciphers "are mirror-images of one
  another as to content".
- Supporting observation: both ciphers share a symbol used nowhere else, the "inverted umbrella"
  (character No. 10 of the April 1970 cipher, No. 8 of the June 1970 cipher).
- Claims: "definitively answers the question as to whether the Zodiac's cipher-system has ever been
  cracked."
- Citation: The Cryptogram Nov-Dec 1986, scan page 2 (p.4) bottom and scan page 3 (p.10).

## Puzzle-relevant remarks

- **How Penn thinks a hidden message should be built**: everything said is literally true ("whatever the
  Zodiac said, no matter how goofy-sounding, was true"); meaning is carried by number, not words
  ("a means of translating linguistic expressions into number"); a valid message gives "the same result
  two or more different ways" - redundancy "proves intent ... design and that the author is not insane"
  (MB scan 3; Mensa scan 11 right). The author wants credit without consequences: "how to separate credit
  from consequences" (scan 11 right).
- **"Meta-writing"**: "it would be possible to write a document whose content said one thing and whose
  form said another" (term from *Cambridge 28*, June 1964, Harvard student periodical) (scan 14 right).
  Formats matter more than plaintext: the 408 solution was "a red herring"; "nobody took a close look
  at the format" (MB scan 3).
- **Reading conventions he licenses** (all usable for decoding his own puzzles): Morse with dot=0/dash=1,
  letter boundaries erased and freely redivided; leading zeros droppable; strings may be read backwards
  (Z13; O'HARE reversed = pi); a binary number may be split off and the rest read as Morse ("10" + NINE;
  130 + I); symbols outside the alphabet dropped, signature symbol kept as a separator; "over" =
  division, "before" = subtraction (Roman-numeral logic), adjacency of joined angles = "union"; pictures
  as rebus (X in MASS); dates as day-of-year or as decimal digits M-D-YY; ages in days; decimal digits
  regrouped as alphabet numbers (13,880 -> M H H O); coordinates rounded to the nearest whole degree;
  rectangle R x C = "R times C"; letter counts, character positions (#100, #117, #213) and mm distances
  all count as numbers; casting out nines (130 -> 4).
- **Self-made signals**: Penn ends "The Radian Ciphers" with "xxx", explicitly equating it with Morse
  01010 "end of message" (CG86 p.10). The 1985 cover letter is a reader challenge: "I will give you the
  material which you can use to come up with a name on your own. The answers are no farther away than the
  nearest public library with a half-decent reference collection." Mensa readers could get more by
  sending a SASE (MB p.25 editor's note).
- **Probability claims** (style he uses to "prove" design): 1/17^3 x 1/2^8 ~ 1 in 1.25 million;
  roulette 55,000:1, royal flush 650,000:1; 20 of 200 million = 1 in 10 million (scan 9 right, 12 right).
- **Favourite numbers/keys in these sources**: 17 (grid width, TIMES 17, titwillo 17 mm), 24 (24x17,
  24 cm x 17 cm), 38 (TIME; 38-letter first sentence; 3800 block; 38th birthday; age 38; 38 deg; 38th

  pi and its binary fraction 0.0010010000111 (13 places), radian 57.2957795131 / 57 deg 17' 44.80625"
