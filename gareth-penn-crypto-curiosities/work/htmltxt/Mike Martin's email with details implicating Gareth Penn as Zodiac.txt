Here's the long answer. 

If I remember correctly (caveat:  it's been a long time) the "O"Hare as surrogate that Penn could safely relive the crimes through" idea was suggested years ago on the first [LINK http://ZodiacKiller.com] ZodiacKiller.com board, by a group that included a well-known cryptographer named Glen Claston (Google him for more info).   This was back in the days when what I consider more experienced folks hashed over the Zodiac case, relying less on codes, ciphers,  and the "POI of the Week" and more on just good old fashioned gum-shoeing to either zero in -- or eliminate -- POIs.   

The forensic psychiatrist featured in my "Unfriendly Fire" feature story, Mike Jones, confirmed this surrogate possibility.  He's well acquainted with the Penn/O'Hare saga.

[LINK http://weeklyscientist.blogspot.com/2013/11/weekly-scientist-story-leads-to-major.html] http://weeklyscientist.blogspot.com/2013/11/weekly-scientist-story-leads-to-major.html

Penn was of great interest "back in the day" because of his well-known "radian theory" -- and because he was such an asshole, scolding, berating, correcting, and even threatening -- by letter and email -- those he felt were misinterpreting, misappropriating, or whatever his work.    

This early group was among the first to notice that Penn bore more resemblance to the Zodiac killer than O'Hare.   I think it was Claston who discovered that Penn's father Hugh was an Army Air Corps cryptographer during WW II (later confirmed by Quinton Hegner, Hugh's partner at the California Crime Statistics branch of Cal Highway Patrol; military records; Penn himself; and family).   O'Hare had no connection to cryptography that anyone -- even Penn -- could find, and yet Penn kept insisting that O'Hare was the brains behind all Z's crypto stuff.  

Continuing this line of reasoning, Jake Wark and Ed Neil discovered -- I have no idea how -- that Penn cheated on his wife with then 27-year-old Maddie Borges, Stella Borges' niece (later confirmed by close friends; Borges' ex; and Penn himself).   This is a direct link to the Zodiac killings that Mike O'Hare never had.  

Gareth and Maddie (Madeleine) -- a lifelong Vallejoan -- went way back, I later discovered, to at least 1972, when he -- believe it or not -- officiated her wedding to Bob Stiglitz when she was ~22.   Penn had some kind of minister's license then -- I don't recall what religion.   Before he died, Bob and I spoke about all this.  He had divorced Maddie years earlier.   Penn, meanwhile, "was probably the most unusual guy I ever knew," Bob told me.  "He really stood out."    

Just as you're thinking "WTF?" Penn's wife Mary Ann started working at People's Continuation High School in Vallejoin August 1968, after they had both returned from Europe.   Though they lived in Berkeley, Gareth often drove her to school because they only had one car.   Mary Ann worked at People's Continuation High until the following school year (Sept. 1969).   Meanwhile, Penn is trying to show that O'Hare was working in San Francisco sometime between 1967-68, giving him at best only a tangential connection to the Vallejo-Benicia-Napa area, where Penn spent much of his young adult life.  

Over the years, people have continued investigating this line of fact-based reasoning -- no ciphers required.  While Penn was insisting O'Hare was a member of the Harvard Rifle Team, and therefore had firearms experience, Penn's military records showed that he was an Expert Marksman, receiving that designation in 1965.  

It was Penn's mother Jean who was the crazy schizophrenic living in a Tenderloin district flop house after she divorced his abusive stepfather Miles -- for the second time.  O'Hare, by comparison, had a model childhood.  

It was both Penn's father and stepfather who were in the Army Air Corps and Penn who was in the reserves with Bay Area base privileges.   All would have had access to Wingwalker shoes of the sort found at Lake Berryessa.  O'Hare and his family had nothing to do with the military.    

Penn was the Old Norse/Old Icelandic linguistics expert, not O'Hare.  Penn was always quoting Gilbert and Sullivan and their imitators, Flanders and Swann, not O'Hare.    And so forth, such that the chart below was assembled.  

On the Opord site, one of the posters visited People's Continuation High, and drove the route to the Benicia and Vallejo murder sites from it (Google image it).   In 2012, I visited it myself, and drove the route.   The high school is isolated today, just before a dead end and a park, not far from the old railway tracks.  45 years ago, it was REALLY isolated.    When I asked Penn if he had Mary Ann's keys and could use the deserted school on school holidays, when the first two murders were committed, he flew off the handle.   I thought it a great place to change blood-spattered clothes.   

Age and Ethnicity

Gareth Penn is a Caucasian male with light features. He was born in 1941, which makes him the right age (27-29) andright ethnicity.

Obsessed with case for 30 years

Like an arsonist revisiting his fires, Penn has obsessively revisited the Zodiac case for 30 years. He publicly accused U.C. Berkeley public policy professor Mike O'Hare of the crimes, launching a Zodiac-style harassment campaignagainst O'Hare with cards, letters, and cryptic notes. He authored two books about the Zodiac case, Times 17: The Amazing Story of the Zodiac Murders in California and Massachusetts, 1966-1981, and The Second Power: A Mathematical Analysis of the Letters Attributed to the Zodiac Murderer.

The latter book gets down and dirty, accusing O'Hare's mother of incest. 

Affair with Madeleine Borges in Vallejo

Penn has admitted to a long-time love affair with Madeleine Borges, the niece of Stella Borges, the woman who found Zodiac murder victims 

David Faraday and Betty Lou Jensen. Coincidentally, Madeleine Borges was also high school classmate of David Faraday. 

Language skills

Penn graduated from UC Berkeley in 1962 with Honors in Medieval German and later, a Master's Degree in Medieval German. Like the Zodiac,

he has a command of linguistics. In a Mensa Ecphorizer article entitled Lima Riki, Penn notes that "in a previous incarnation," he used to write limericks "in Old Norse." Penn also made several contributions to Tom Burnam's 1980 book,More Misinformation, including an at-length discussion of the ancient Norse poem Edda and its author, 12th century Norse historian Snorri Sturluson.  His Berkeley transcripts confirm he took courses in Old Norse and Old Icelandic languages.  

A Zodiac letter Penn later commented on mentions Old Norse writing, used in the word "sla." 

Surveying/radian/map-making/military experience

Penn became an Artillery Survey Instructor for the US Army in 1965-67. A U.S. Army artillery surveyor performs astronomical observations; measures azimuths, grid coordinates, and angles on maps; and operates/maintains vehicles, radios, weapons, and other survey equipment in support of artillery operations. Penn remained in the Army reserves from 1967-71 while living in Berkeley and Vallejo. 

Expert Marksmanship

In the Army, Penn received the highest marksmanship designation available: Expert.

Cryptography knowledge

Penn was very close to his father Hugh Scott Penn, a U.S. Army cryptographer during World War II. Gareth Penn has also written at least one serious academic paper about cryptography, which he presented at a conference during the 1990s.

Criminology knowledge

Hugh Penn also worked for the California Department of Justice was an early criminal "profiler." He wrote a report about the so-called "East Area Rapist" who tormented Sacramento and Northern California in the 1970s. As "Hugh S. Penn," he also authored several studies of both juvenile criminal behavior, and -- as an associate researcher for the California Highway Patrol -- several auto accident studies. Penn writes that his father collaborated with him on the Zodiac case. 

Knowledge of poetry and the arts

Penn's mother Jean Sewell Standish was a noted poet who regularly published her works in The New Yorker, Harper's, and The Atlantic Monthly.

Interest in Opera

Penn and his wife Mary Ann -- to whom he was married during the murders -- were ardent, long-time fans and supporters of the opera. They held season tickets to the San Francisco Opera they later fought over during their divorce. They also donated money and time to that organization.

Interest in Gilbert and Sullivan's opera The Mikado

As the Zodiac killer did in his letters to the press, Penn frequently cites Gilbert and Sullivan in his own writing. In Lima Rikifrom the

Ecphorizer, Penn notes that, "While some purists insist on pure rhyme, a contrived or near rhyme, especially in the pointe, heightens the humorous effect, a technique well known to fans of Ogden Nash, Sir William S. Gilbert, and the shaggy-dog story."

In a Time Magazine letter to the editor regarding a review of The Mikado in which the reviewer mistakenly attributed the lyrics to 

Sullivan and the music to Gilbert, Penn wrote: "Let's see, now. Sullivan wrote the words, and Gilbert wrote the music. Holmes is the doctor, and Watson the detective. Harpo had the cigar, and Groucho tooted the auto horn. Thanks for setting the record straight."

Penn has also signed his emails with a quote from a British duo known for their Gilbert and Sullivan parodies, Flanders and Swann: "But people have always eaten people! What else is there to eat? If the Juju had meant us not to eat people, he wouldn't have made them of meat!"

Knowledge of The Most Dangerous Game

A former student of Mary Ann Penn wrote on [LINK http://RateMyTeachers.com] RateMyTeachers.com that The Most Dangerous Game was a classroom favorite Mary Ann often taught her English classes. Zodiac frequently referenced this short novel in his taunting letters.

Admiration of the Zodiac Killer

In Portrait of the Artist as a Mass Murderer, a 1981 story he wrote for New West Magazine, Penn called the Zodiac an artistic genius engaged in a "murderous art project. Other artists had sought to remove their work from the ordinary human perspective. Zodiac trumped them all," Penn wrote. He also expressed great admiration for the "wonderful mind" of Michael O'Hare, the man he accused of the crimes.

Placing himself in the Zodiac Crimes

In Times 17, Penn audaciously laid out a scenario whereby, as duty person in charge of daily roll call, he could have falsified an entry and left Fort Sill in October, 1966, taken a military hop to March AFB, and killed Cheri Jo Bates in nearby Riverside. 

Oddly enough, he was actually in San Francisco that exact month. On leave from Fort Sill, he attended a going away party for a friend and met his second wife Mary Ann. Why didn't he mention this in Times 17??

 Why the Long-time Beard?

In an appearance on a WE Television's Case Reopened: The Zodiac, Gareth Penn wore glasses and a beard. The Zodiac made reference to a disguise that could have been a beard in his "bomb letter, i.e., where he was clean-shaven "like the description" on the wanted posters when he did his "thing," but having disguise -- perhaps a full beard -- otherwise. 

On the night of her murder, Riverside police connected Zodiac victim Cheri Jo Bates to a young man, about 25 years of age, wearing a brown beard. 

Letters-to-the-editor

Gareth Penn's astute, officious, and often biting letters to the editors of Scientific American, Nature, The Economist, at least 15 letters over the years to National Public Radio and even the San Francisco Chronicle have tantalized amateur sleuths as another similarity between Penn and Zodiac. 

Berkeley's Blue Meanies

In one of his letters, the Zodiac killer referenced the Blue Meanies: Alameda County sheriff deputies responsible for Berkeley's Bloody Thursday People's Park riot. Penn was also familiar with the Blue Meanies.

"In May of 1969, I was standing on the front steps of the duplex in Berkeley which I shared with a person named Gerard," Penn wrote in a Mensa Ecphorizer article entitled Where Were You?. "Gerard and I were both watching a National Guard helicopter circling over the campus of the University of California, which had been the center, over the previous week, of the so-called People's Park disturbances."

Long time librarian

Zodiac victim Cheri Jo Bates was killed outside the Riverside City College library just after studying there. Penn was a long-time librarian.

Residence and work in Vallejo and Zodiac areas

Penn's second wife, Mary Ann Winterrowd, taught for nearly 20 years in the Vallejo Unified School District (1968-1988), spending most of that time at Solano Junior High School. They lived in Vallejo in 1971, and Penn directed the Donovan J. McCune Room at the Vallejo Public Library from 1972-79. Penn has lived in Berkeley; Tiburon, Vallejo, Stinson Beach, Corte Real, San Rafael, Larkspur, Corte Madera; and Napa, Calif.

SCA-style Hood

Penn was a long-time member of the Society for Creative Anachronism, or SCA, a medieval re-enactment society founded in Berkeley by the author Diana Paxson in 1966. The Zodiac wore a costume/hood reminiscent of an SCA executioner during one of his murders. 

Abusive, dysfunctional family life

Penn reports that his stepfather, Miles Archibald Standish, was extremely abusive. He reports that his mother, Jean Sewell Penn, went insane and died impoverished.
