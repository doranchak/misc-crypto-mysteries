David:

The material below -- from the July and September 1976 issues of San Francisco Magazine -- was forwarded me recently, and thought I'd pass it along.     I notice you're keeping up with Mr. Penn's suggestive reminders from his GZ4216 blog.   

“Strange de Jim” was a pseudonym person or persons used to communicate with the SF Chronicle columnist Herb Caen, whom the Zodiac Killer referenced in one or more of his letters, (e.g. “Tell herb caen I am here, I have always been here.”).    I don’t know why San Francisco Magazine would compare Mr. Penn to Strange de Jim, but apparently they did.    A copy of that article might be an interesting read.    

Mr. Penn notes that he was some sort of contributor to Caen, but then stopped, so Caen’s columns may have some Penn writings.    Read why Penn says he stopped writing to Caen, and compare it to Z’s “Dripping Pen” card:    “Sorry I haven’t written, but I just washed my pen…and i can’t do a thing with it!”

It‘s interesting too that de Jim’s note references signs of the Zodiac.   

I suspect if Mr. Penn was writing about Strange de Jim in 1976 (while he was still at the Vallejo library, not long before his affair with Maddie Borges), there may some fertile exploratory material in de Jim's notes and memos to Caen, for the Z-aficionados on the Morford board.     

Happy Holidays,

Mike

San Francisco Magazine - Volume 18 -- 1976

Letter from Gareth Penn to San Francisco Magazine, re:  Strange de Jim profile

On the basis of certain superficial similarities between us suggested by the Strange de Jim feature appearing in your July issue, some people have erroneously concluded that I am in fact he, or that he is in fact I, as the case may be,  a conclusion to which I take the most strenuous exception.   I would like to take this opportunity to state categorically that I am not now. nor have I ever been, Strange de Jim.    Moreover, while I have from time to time been as Strange or even Stranger, I have never been as Often.   In any case, I am not such a shameless anonymity-hound as he is, as everyone knows.    PS.  Please spell my name right.   I stopped contributing to Caen when he left out a letter once.   If you're careful, I could throw some business your way.  -- Gareth S. Penn

Letter from “Strange de Jim”, re:  Gareth Penn

In your September issue, you published a letter from Gareth S. Penn claiming that he is not I.   I believe that he is wrong.   In the first place, he admits that there are superficial similarities.   Anyone who has ever known me will testify that all my similarities are superficial.   In the second place, he is not listed in the San Francisco telephone directory, and neither am I.   In the third place, neither of us bears the slightest resemblance to Robert Redford.   I would be happy to discuss the matter further with Mr. Penn as soon as I return from Chico where I am going to undergo the world's first sign change operation (I was born a Cancer, but I've always known I am really a Leo).   --- His Abandonment, Strange de Jim

David:  

Ray Grant contacted me, which prompted me to check out your post.  Quite a few adds!   Here’s something that might make the Z-connection more clear, complete with relevant links:     

From the 1976 San Francisco Magazine letters between Gareth Penn and “Strange de Jim”, we learn that:

1)  Penn has written to SF Chronicle columnist Herb Caen so many times, San Francisco Magazine confused him with Strange de Jim in a story the letters reference.    Caen published letters from readers, so the letters from Gareth would have had “Gareth Penn” on them.   

2)  Writing under the pseudonym, Strange de Jim was also a prolific contributor to Herb Caen – maybe the most prolific.     

[LINK https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herb_Caen] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herb_Caen

3)  The Zodiac referenced Caen in one of his letters  (“Tell herb caen, I am here. I have always been here.”)    [LINK http://i70.servimg.com/u/f70/14/94/97/81/zodiac23.jpg] http://i70.servimg.com/u/f70/14/94/97/81/zodiac23.jpg

4)  In the San Fran Mag letters, Gareth tells how he quit writing to Herb Caen because Caen left a letter off his last name, e.g. writing something like “I just got another letter from my good friend Gareth Pen”.   

5)  The Zodiac sent a card to the Chronicle, where Herb Caen worked, that said, "Sorry I haven't written, but I just washed my pen..."

[LINK http://www.zodiackiller.com/340Card.html] http://www.zodiackiller.com/340Card.html

The Zodiac letter suggests Z was connected with Caen somehow, ended the connection, but wanted Caen to know he was still around.  The Zodiac card suggests Z was writing, but stopped for some reason.   It contains what could be interpreted as a pun on Penn’s name.  The missing letter “n” jives with the reason Penn said he stopped writing to Caen.   Penn is a trained linguist, so the pun and taking offense to the lost letter in his name is right up his alley.  

To me, this is pretty straightforward, but I realize it may not be for others.    I have not read the SF Mag story that references Penn and Caen, but hope to find it, time-permitting.

Thanks for posting that.  Yes, I do have a good timeline of Penn's activities around the times of the Zodiac crimes.  An excellent one, in fact.   Will endeavor to send an abbreviated version.  

Looked at the Ross Sullivan timeline.  Is he a new POI?   If so, my sources would want to know:

Does he have a strong connection to Vallejo?   I see some stuff about a San Bernardino mental hospital and lots of time living in Santa Cruz.  These would not be Z-case geo-locations.   Read Kim Rossmo’s Zodiac Geoprofile for more.     

A strong humanities education with good if not top grades?   As Northeastern University criminology professor and serial profiling expert Jack Levin said, “The Zodiac case may have been the most cerebral murder case of all time.”  If you don’t have a super-educated, super-intelligent POI, you don’t have the killer. 

Demonstrated interest in the theatre and arts?   The Zodiac’s many references to pieces like The Mikado are diagnostic for this trait.  

Expert firearms training?   Ken Narlow emphasized how important this was when I interviewed him.  In fact, it was the only reason he thought Penn might have something with O'Hare, who had been on the Harvard Rifle Team.  Narlow was shocked to hear Penn had received his expert marksman designation in the Army, just three years before the killings started.    

History doing any of things Z did, like writing letters to the editor or making cryptograms?   These weren't newly learned or newly-used skills for the killer, but almost second nature, my sources say.    

Any indication of family life trauma, abusing animals, etc.?    Common part of many serial profiles.  Both true with Gareth.  

Post-crime involvement in the case?   Looking for someone who inserted himself or herself into the crimes after they were committed was a frequent recommendation I received, after presenting on Penn/O’Hare at the Midwest Criminal Justice Association annual conference and the Southern Criminal Justice Association annual conference.   [LINK http://www.mcja.org/] http://www.mcja.org    [LINK http://www.scja.net/] http://www.scja.net

Evidence of past involvement with Herb Caen would be a strong new piece of material to Columbia College criminology professor Barry Lankford.  I worked with one of his students on a Z-project that examined “coincidences” with other old records.  The missing letter beef would be like icing on the cake.    “There are few, if any, coincidences in crime,” he once said. 

[LINK http://spotlight.ccis.edu/2009/09/zodiac.html] http://spotlight.ccis.edu/2009/09/zodiac.html

I’m a journalist, and so I go with what my sources tell me and where they point me.    They’ve pointed me to Penn, so sure – that’s where I’ve been looking. 

(BTW, none have expressed any interest in the ciphers, except as they indicate an expertise and interest of the killer’s).   

I like Ray and get along with him just fine.  That said, I don’t attack him, but take the good stuff he’s offered, which is quite a bit of hard evidence of Penn’s past activities and writings.    Ray is a near-70 year old retired postal worker whose classmates in Pittsburgh remembered this brilliant kind of misfit guy, who has taken on all this new technology at his age – Kindle publishing (not easy); online forums; etc.   I admire that.   His big flaw is that he doesn’t take being goaded well, and both on Amazon and in the forums, he was goaded and belittled endlessly.   He fights back.  So fucking what?  

People need to chill out.  No amateur sleuth is going to solve this case – it’s way too cold – so who really cares if someone has a far-out theory?    Theories in this case are WAY over-abundant, many WAY out there, and their champions legion.   Assholes are also legion -- look at Voigt and Butterfingers.

Interesting.  Let me preface by saying it’s very cool that the Z-aficionado community digs up these old documents, and studies them for information about potential suspects.  

Since I’m not a criminologist, I’ll begin this as a journalist being presented with a story about two potential homicide suspects:  Ross Sullivan and Gareth Penn.  Our newspaper has limited resources, so my editor says we must go with the strongest one.  

We start with the Toschi/librarian letters about Ross.   The Toschi letter I’m seeing is to him, not from him, btw.   It dashes off a quick mention of Ross Sullivan from a Dave Peterson.  Who is he?  My first thought is “so what”? 

The Jo Ann Bailey librarian letter portrays a smallish group of women concerned by a guy they – but not the younger women, from what she says – consider creepy.   Liberal use of the words “possibly,” “apparently,” and “probably” inspire further shrugs.   Again, so what? 

Does Sullivan have a strong connection to Vallejo?   No.   Gap in his whereabouts?   But Penn has multiple strong Vallejo connections before, during, and after the homicides.   Spend time trying to close this decades old gap – or go with Gareth?  

A strong humanities education with good if not top grades?   I’m looking for a POI with such a strong command of the Mikado, he can dash off a wicked parody of it.   And quote it.
