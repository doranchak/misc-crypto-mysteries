Cultural use of advanced tech tools: a homophonic substitution cipher

Source: r/codes thread by u/ChiameraKrther. Status: solved (three cells are judgment calls, see below).

“A code abt the cultural use of advanced tech tools. Substitution encryption, but with 3 variants for each, and +1 for vowels.” — u/ChiameraKrther
Result. The 507 glyphs (12 rows of 42, plus 3 on the last row) are a letter-for-glyph substitution in which every consonant has up to 3 different glyphs and every vowel up to 4. The text is a short opinion piece about AI. The cipher gives no spaces or punctuation, and its spellings are kept exactly as decoded (SHOULDNT, PRECIVED, OCCURE, RELIGOUS, HAPPEEN).

How the cipher works

  • Homophonic substitution. Each plaintext letter is replaced by one of several glyphs for that letter. The glyphs of one letter look nothing alike, so shape families tell you nothing.
  • 3 variants for each, +1 for vowels. That is what the glyph counts show. No consonant uses more than 3 glyphs and no vowel more than 4, and all five vowels use all four of theirs. Letters that are rare in the text (B, J, K, X, Z) show fewer than 3 because the other variants never came up. Q does not occur.
  • The variant is chosen freely. I tested for a fixed rotation (first A uses glyph 1, second A glyph 2, and so on) and found none. The choice looks free: a letter's variants turn up in no fixed order.
  • Number glyphs. Five glyphs are numbers followed by a period: 12. = A, 15. = E, 40. = I, 99. = V, 33. = X. They are ordinary variants, not a separate layer. Other glyphs are digits or letters too (3 = O, 2 = M, 8 = P, A = N, K = F, W = Z, B = U, X = L). The look of a glyph says nothing about its value.

Judgment calls and caveats

Three cells that are not clean reads

  • Row 9, col 19 (TRI_OVERWRITTEN): Filled triangle drawn over an earlier glyph (legs of the old glyph show underneath). By shape it is TRI (s); read here as m so that the text says MYTHS. Letter by shape alone: S; read as: M.
  • Row 9, col 20 (SCRIBBLE): Looping scribble, read as a struck-out cell and skipped. By shape alone it matches no other glyph (a lone "Q"-like spiral). Letter by shape alone: B; read as: (SKIPPED).
  • Row 12, col 27 (DASH_THICK): One long, thick bar instead of a thin dash. Read as a merged "=" (EQ, h), because TECHNOLOGICAL needs an h here and the ordinary dash is l. Letter by shape alone: L; read as: H.

If you prefer the strictly literal reading (no interpretation), cells 9.19 and 9.20 decode by shape to S and B, giving “RELIGOUS SBYTHS”, and 12.27 decodes to L, giving “TECLNOLOGICAL”. The main reading resolves all three from the surrounding words. TECHNOLOGICAL and the digit-free spelling around it are certain; MYTHS is my interpretation of two messy cells.

Glyphs I treated as the same symbol

  • Row 10, col 41: Drawn like STEP4; merged with STEP2 (same glyph, different wobble).
  • Row 11, col 27: Drawn like TRIBAR; merged with TRIOUT (same glyph, different wobble).

What I could not check

  • Reddit blocked automated access, so I never saw the thread or any answer from the poster. The decoding stands on the image alone.
  • The poster's own spelling quirks are kept. A few look like slips by the encoder: HAPPEEN (ne swapped), OCCURE (extra E), RELIGOUS (missing I), PRECIVED (for “perceived”).
  • Unused glyph variants (the third B, the third J, and so on) cannot be listed, since they never appear in this text.

How I solved it

  1. Transcription. Deskewed the photo, removed the grid lines, grouped pen strokes into glyphs and checked every row visually until each had exactly 42 cells. Then clustered glyph shapes (chamfer distance) and reviewed all 507 cells by eye against enlarged crops, which fixed about 30 mislabelled cells.
  2. Cipher type. The index of coincidence of the glyph stream is 0.018. English split over 3 glyphs per consonant and 4 per vowel predicts 0.019, as the hint says. (A plain monoalphabetic substitution would give about 0.065.)
  3. Automatic solve. Simulated annealing with a 4-gram English model and a cap of 3 (consonant) or 4 (vowel) glyphs per letter. With most glyph classes right it converged on readable text (“the technology of AI shouldn't be a thing to fear...”), though with several glyphs still assigned the wrong letter. It helped to make the cap one-sided (a letter may use fewer variants than allowed, but never more), since rare letters do not show all of theirs. scripts/solve.c is the solver.
  4. Finishing by hand. Fixed the wrong classes from context: K-glyph = F (“to fear”), lollipop = W (“wasted”, “why we”), three linked discs = Y (“worrying”), outline triangle = M (“time”, “prime”), W-glyph = Z (“catastrophizing”), Б = J (“reject”), and so on. After that every glyph obeys the 3/4 variant limit with no exceptions.
