How the code works

Each letter is turned into its alphabet number (A=1, B=2, …, Z=26) and multiplied by its position in the message (1–48). So the ninth letter, S (19), is written as 9 × 19 = 171. To decrypt, divide each value by its position. Because the multiplier is the position itself, the same letter gets a different number almost every time. The two E's in “SOMETIMES” at positions 4 and 8 become 20 and 40. Simple frequency counting doesn't work against this.

The “transposed, inspired by Z340” part is where the 48 positions go in the 8×6 grid. The pattern below is the same for both drawings:

Position key, shaded by 12-letter block.
Row 1 is the top row, as drawn.

The layout is the same move repeated at three levels: split into two halves, then interleave them.

  1. Blocks. The 48 letters form four blocks of 12. Blocks 1 and 2 fill the bottom four rows and blocks 3 and 4 fill the top four.
  2. Rows. Inside a block, the letters zig-zag between two rows that are two rows apart, like a two-rail fence. Odd-numbered letters go on the lower rail and even-numbered letters on the upper one. The two blocks in each half interleave their rails, so reading upward from the bottom the rows are: block 1 odd, block 2 odd, block 1 even, block 2 even. The top half repeats this with blocks 3 and 4.
  3. Columns. Inside a row, its six letters fill columns 1, 3, 5 and then 2, 4, 6.

As a formula, for a 0-based position p = 12B + 2j + e (block B = 0–3, rail e = 0–1, slot j = 0–5):
row from bottom = 4·⌊B/2⌋ + 2e + (B mod 2), column = 2·(j mod 3) + ⌊j/3⌋.
This reproduces all 48 positions of the commenter's key exactly. As in Z340, the letters are written down in a different order from the one they are read in, but the pattern is different. Z340's transposition moved diagonally, one row down and two columns right. This one interleaves.

Checks

Every cell where something doesn't fit

The commenter worked from the Reddit post's typed numbers and reported “fixing what seems to be 3 errors” in the first grid. Compared with the drawing, two of those three are typos in the typed copy. Only one is a slip in the art itself. The second grid has one slip that the comment doesn't mention.

ArtCell (row, col)PosReddit textDrawingShould beVerdict
1row 1, col 446320230230 = 46×5 (E)typo in the post; the drawing is correct (the E of HELL)
1row 8, col 49161171171 = 9×19 (S)typo in the post; the drawing is correct (the last S of the first SOMETIMES)
1row 7, col 219113113133 = 19×7 (G)slip in the art: 113 ÷ 19 = 5.95. The nearest whole letter is F (114), but only G makes “GHOST”
2row 2, col 232763763736 = 32×23 (W)slip in the art: last two digits swapped, 763 ÷ 32 = 23.84 (“I WAS”)

The other 94 cells divide exactly and give a letter from A to Z. Both messages are exactly 48 letters long, with nothing left over.

The commenter's quick tests, re-run

The parity test catches the 763 slip by itself. Row 2 holds only even positions, so every value there must be even, and 763 is odd. The 113 slip sits at an odd position in an odd row, so parity can't catch it.

A note on “ALLWAYS”

The first message is 48 letters with nothing added (23 + 25). The second message spelled “ALWAYS” would be 12 + 18 + 17 = 47 letters. The doubled L in the second “ALLWAYS” (positions 36 and 37, drawn as 432 = 36 × 12 and 444 = 37 × 12) brings it to exactly 48. The first “ALWAYS” at positions 20–25 has one L. It's impossible to say whether the extra L was a deliberate filler or a spelling slip that happened to fill the grid, but it is the 48th letter.
