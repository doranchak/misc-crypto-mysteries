How the cipher works — and why the S-box doesn't help

What crypt.py does

Let L be the secret permutation of the 95 printable ASCII symbols (the S-box). Write xi = L(plaintexti) and yi = L(ciphertexti). Encryption is

yi = xi + xi−1 (mod 95) x−1 = init

The S-box only relabels symbols. Underneath it is an ordinary autokey recurrence over ℤ95 — a linear system wearing a mask.

The leak

Unrolling the recurrence gives a closed form that depends on the ciphertext only through L:

xi = (−1)i · ( Ai − init ), Ai = Σt≤i (−1)t · L(ct)

So the plaintext label at position i is a signed sum of ciphertext labels. Because addition is commutative, bigrams ab and ba also encrypt to the same symbol.

The attack in four steps

1

Repeats leak equalities

If a ciphertext substring of length m repeats, the aligned plaintext labels differ by a constant ±d. For real English d = 0: the plaintext repeated too. (All 22 copies of ciphertext VdP/ turned out to be the plaintext " the ".)

2

Each equality is linear

xi = xj is one homogeneous linear equation in the 95 unknown labels plus init. Its coefficients are read straight off the ciphertext. Repeats of ≥ 5 characters give 438 equations.

3

Solve mod 5 and mod 19

95 = 5 · 19, so Gaussian elimination runs over two small fields. Rank 94 of 96 in both, leaving a 2-dimensional nullspace: the true key plus a meaningless "all labels equal" direction.

4

Rank 95 shifts, pick English

The meaningless direction leaves 5 × 19 = 95 shifted keys. Decode all, score with a character-trigram model: one is English, the other 94 are noise.

How fast the unknowns collapse

The "unknown dimension" is the size of the nullspace — how many free parameters remain in the key. 2 means fully determined (true key + the harmless constant direction).

Caveats and honest notes

  • The key is recovered up to scaling. If (L, init) is a key then (k·L mod 95, k·init mod 95) is too, for any k coprime to 95 — 72 equivalent keys, all verified to decrypt identically. The attack cannot tell which one the author drew, and it does not recover the passphrase string (Python seeds its generator from a hash of it), but that is not needed to decrypt.
  • The posted ciphertext is 3,792 characters, not ~5,000, and the plaintext stops mid-sentence ("…I can autograph that. A little"). Everything posted is decoded.
  • The ciphertext was used exactly as pasted, including its 10 double-backslashes. It decrypts perfectly, so no escaping was lost.
  • "ourjob" (no space) appears in the recovered text. Re-encryption reproduces the ciphertext exactly, so that quirk was in the text fed to the cipher, not a decoding error.
  • Neither the sample key nor the sample initial_shift in the repository was used, and nothing about the plaintext was assumed. The lead-in line V sbyybjrq gur ehyrf is ROT13 for "I followed the rules" and played no part.
  • The English-scoring step only needs to pick one of 95 shifts. The best scored −2.32 and the runner-up −4.73, so the choice is not close; the decode is also visibly the Bee Movie script.
